\begin{abstract}
LLM-based agents increasingly execute multi-step tasks conditioned on a structured profile of user constraints. When such an agent adopts an incorrect assumption about the user early in its trajectory, the error propagates silently through subsequent steps, producing an output that violates the user's explicitly stated constraints. State-of-the-art self-correction methods such as Reflexion and Tree-of-Thoughts retry the full task or search alternative reasoning paths, yet none identify \emph{which specific assumption} introduced the failure.

We propose \method, a framework that extracts the assumptions an agent relied upon, structures them into a provenance-aware dependency graph, and conducts targeted counterfactual repair experiments to isolate the root-cause assumption and correct only the affected portion of the trajectory.

Across four benchmarks and four LLMs (16 cells; 864 paired observations), \method achieves the highest or tied-highest task success on \textbf{15 of 16} cells with \textbf{zero significant losses} against any of seven baselines. On Profile-injected TruthfulQA $\times$ Mistral 12\,B, success rises from 63.9\% (Direct) to \textbf{86.1\%} (+22.2\,pp); on MuSiQue $\times$ Mistral 12\,B, from 71.7\% to \textbf{91.7\%} (+20.0\,pp). Pooled across the seven-baseline comparison, \method outperforms the baseline mean by \textbf{14.7\,pp}. Code: \url{\repourl}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

LLMs~\cite{ouyang2022instructgpt, openai2023gpt4} deployed as user-facing agents operate over a structured profile of preferences, constraints, and user-specific facts. Because users rarely inspect every intermediate step, a mistake made early in the trajectory propagates silently until it surfaces as a final violation: a restaurant that ignores a dietary restriction, a hotel that conflicts with a stated budget, a recommendation that treats an inferred preference as explicit. We call these \emph{user-specific hallucinations}: outputs that may be internally coherent but violate, overgeneralize, or contradict the user's own stated constraints~\cite{sun2024pfqabench, xie2024travelplanner}. Existing self-correction methods do not directly target this failure mode---Self-Refine~\cite{madaan2023selfrefine} and Reflexion~\cite{shinn2023reflexion} retry forward, Tree-of-Thoughts~\cite{yao2023tot} and Graph-of-Thoughts~\cite{besta2024got} search over completions, and process-level reward methods~\cite{lightman2023verify} flag low-confidence steps but do not localize which latent user assumption to revise. Tyen et al.~\cite{tyen2024selfcorrect,tyen2024llmerrors} make this gap concrete: LLMs can detect errors but consistently fail at \emph{localizing} which step is wrong.

We introduce \method, a framework for counterfactual fault attribution in user-conditioned agent trajectories. \method represents a trajectory as a provenance-typed Assumption-DAG (profile, tool, model-inferred, world) over observable trajectory artifacts. When a profile-grounded violation is detected, the system estimates an attribution score over candidate faulty ancestors using counterfactual repair trials and selects a repair point by trading off fault probability against downstream edit cost; if the post-repair verifier still rejects the trajectory, Sequential Posterior Refinement (SPR; Eq.~\ref{eq:spr-update}) treats that rejection as additional counterfactual evidence and updates the same posterior in closed form, retargeting the next-best ancestor without invoking any auxiliary mechanism. Our contributions are: (i) the \dagg + \method framework that performs typed fault attribution and selective subgraph repair (\S\ref{sec:method}); (ii) a paired evaluation across four benchmarks $\times$ four LLMs with honest statistical methodology (\S\ref{sec:experiments}, \S\ref{sec:results}); and (iii) a regime-structure finding: single-round attribution drives wins on deployment-relevant 7--12\,B models, while SPR is the deciding component on 70\,B models with long-context inputs.

\section{\method}\label{sec:method}

Let $x$ denote a user task and $P = \{p_1, \ldots, p_m\}$ a structured user profile. An agent produces a trajectory $\tau = (s_1, u_1, o_1, \ldots, s_T, u_T, o_T, y)$ with steps $s_t$, actions or tool calls $u_t$, observations $o_t$, and final output $y$. A profile-grounded verifier $V(P, x, \tau, y) \in \{0,1\}$ returns 1 when the trajectory or output violates the profile (a \emph{profile-grounded violation}); a reference-free task-success evaluator $S(x, \tau, y) \in \{0,1\}$ judges task-appropriateness from observable trajectory artifacts without access to ground-truth labels (an LLM-based coherence and constraint-satisfaction check, not the gold-answer substring matcher used for benchmark scoring; \S\ref{sec:experiments}). A profile-grounded violation is a \emph{user-specific hallucination} when at least one assumption used in producing $\tau$ or $y$ is unsupported by, contradicted by, or improperly generalized from $P$.

\paragraph{Assumption-DAG.} \method represents the latent commitments of a trajectory as a directed acyclic graph $G = (A, E)$, where each node $a_i \in A$ is an assumption $a_i = (c_i, \rho_i, t_i, \ell_i)$ with one-sentence commitment $c_i$, provenance $\rho_i \in \{\text{profile}, \text{tool}, \text{model}, \text{world}\}$, trajectory step $t_i$, and confidence $\ell_i \in [0,1]$; edges encode dependency. An LLM-based extractor is prompted with $(P, x, \tau)$ and returns JSON containing each assumption's commitment, step, provenance, profile evidence spans, confidence, and dependency list. Edges are added when one assumption justifies, specializes, or enables another. Cycles, when detected, are resolved by removing the edge with the lowest endpoint-node confidence ($\min(\ell_{\text{source}}, \ell_{\text{target}})$); the cycle-removal rate is logged. Extraction precision and recall on synthetic diagnostics (where the gold graph is known by construction) are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:dag-quality}; full prompt in Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}.

\paragraph{Profile-grounded verification.}\label{sec:verifier} The verifier $V$ combines deterministic predicates (budget, allergy, date, location, forbidden-action) with an LLM-based pass for semantic cases not covered by rules; LLM outputs are calibrated against the rule-based layer on a held-out development split. Each violation event records the violated profile span, affected step, severity, and confidence.

\paragraph{Counterfactual fault attribution.}\label{sec:attribution} Given a violation $v$ at trajectory step $t_v$, the goal is to infer which earlier assumption caused it. Let $F \in A$ denote the latent faulty assumption. Because the true likelihood $p(v \mid F{=}a_i, G, \tau, P)$ is unavailable, \method uses counterfactual repair success as empirical evidence for fault assignment. Let $\mathcal{C}(v; G) = \{a_i \in A \mid t_i \leq t_v, \rho_i \neq \text{profile}, a_i \in \Anc(v;G)\}$ denote the candidate set: ancestors of the violation step, excluding immutable profile-derived assumptions. For each candidate $a_i \in \mathcal{C}(v; G)$, the system samples $M$ minimal repairs and measures whether repairing $a_i$ removes the violation while preserving task success:
\begin{equation}
  r_i = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{j=1}^{M} \mathbf{1}[V(P,x,\tau^{(i,j)},y^{(i,j)})=0] \cdot \mathbf{1}[S(x,\tau^{(i,j)},y^{(i,j)})=1],\label{eq:repair-evidence}
\end{equation}
where $\tau^{(i,j)}$ is the trajectory obtained by repairing $a_i$ under sample $j$. The attribution score combines repair evidence with the provenance prior via prior-weighted normalization:
\begin{equation}
  q_i = \frac{\pi_{\rho_i}\, r_i}{\sum_{a_k \in \mathcal{C}(v;G)} \pi_{\rho_k}\, r_k},\label{eq:attribution}
\end{equation}
where $\pi_{\rho_i}$ is a provenance-dependent prior ($\pi_{\text{model}} > \pi_{\text{world}} > \pi_{\text{tool}}$; values in Table~\ref{tab:hparams}). When $\sum_k \pi_{\rho_k} r_k = 0$ (no counterfactual sample removed the violation), we fall back to prior-only attribution $q_i = \pi_{\rho_i} / \sum_k \pi_{\rho_k}$. We refer to $q_i$ as an \emph{attribution score} rather than a calibrated posterior: $r_i$ is a Monte Carlo estimate from intervention trials, not a calibrated likelihood, and $q$ is not calibrated to the true generative process.

\paragraph{Min-cost graph-edit repair.}\label{sec:mcgr}

For candidate $a_i$, let $D_i$ be the downstream subgraph and $C(a_i, G)$ the normalized edit cost:
\begin{equation}
  C(a_i, G) = w_n \frac{|D_i|}{|A|} + w_t \frac{\hat{T}_i}{T_{\max}} + w_c \frac{\hat{K}_i}{K_{\max}} + w_p \,\mathbf{1}[a_i \text{ precedes a verified tool observation}],\label{eq:edit-cost}
\end{equation}
where $|D_i|$ is the number of downstream assumptions to re-execute, $\hat{T}_i$ and $\hat{K}_i$ are estimated regenerated-token and re-issued-tool-call counts (derived by summing per-step token and tool-call counts of $D_i$ from the original trajectory), $T_{\max}$ and $K_{\max}$ are the original trajectory's total token and tool-call counts (used for normalization), and the final term penalizes edits that may invalidate already-verified tool observations. \method selects
\begin{equation}
  a^\star = \arg\min_{a_i \in \mathcal{C}(v;G)} \bigl[-q_i + \lambda\, C(a_i, G)\bigr],\label{eq:mcgr}
\end{equation}
abstaining or falling back to full regeneration when $\max_i q_i < \delta$. We call this the \emph{cost-regularized attribution objective}: it minimizes a regularized score under the approximate attribution score $q$ and cost $C$. The valid prefix before the repair node is preserved; only the downstream subgraph is regenerated.

\paragraph{Sequential Posterior Refinement (SPR).} When the post-repair verifier rejects $\tau^{\text{repair}}$, that rejection is partial evidence against $a^\star$: it is consistent with both ``wrong candidate'' and ``right candidate, bad repair''. Rather than permanently excluding tried candidates, we down-weight them by a Bayesian decay factor $\alpha \in [0,1)$ and renormalize:
\begin{equation}
  q^{(t+1)}_i = \begin{cases} \alpha \cdot q^{(t)}_i \big/ Z^{(t)} & a_i \in \mathcal{T}^{(t)} \\ q^{(t)}_i \big/ Z^{(t)} & \text{otherwise,} \end{cases} \quad Z^{(t)} = \alpha \!\!\sum_{a_j \in \mathcal{T}^{(t)}}\! q^{(t)}_j + \!\!\sum_{a_j \notin \mathcal{T}^{(t)}}\! q^{(t)}_j,\label{eq:spr-update}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{T}^{(t)}$ is the set of candidates whose repair $V$ has already rejected. We use $\alpha = 0.1$ throughout, frozen on the Profile-X dev split (Appendix~\ref{app:hparams}); the second-round selector is insensitive to $\alpha \in [0, 0.3]$ at our cell sizes. We then re-select $a^\star$ via Eq.~\ref{eq:mcgr} on $q^{(t+1)}$, re-execute, and re-verify; the loop runs for at most $T_{\text{spr}}$ rounds. SPR is strictly sequential abductive inference over the same Assumption-DAG and posterior framework as the first round---no auxiliary model, no verbal critique, no full re-generation. Disabling SPR ($T_{\text{spr}}=0$) reverts \method to the targeted-repair-only configuration (Appendix~\ref{app:spr-impl}, Table~\ref{tab:spr-impact}).

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{\method (sketch). Full pseudocode with caching, abstention, and SPR loop in Appendix~\ref{app:spr-impl}.}\label{alg:intro-specter-mini}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \Require task $x$, profile $P$, trajectory $\tau$, verifier $V$
  \State Build \dagg $G$; if $V$ accepts $\tau$ \textbf{return} $\tau$.
  \State $\mathcal{C}(v;G) \gets$ non-profile ancestors of the violated step; score each via Eqs.~\ref{eq:repair-evidence}--\ref{eq:attribution}.
  \For{$t = 0, \ldots, T_{\text{spr}}$}
    \State $a^\star \gets \arg\min_{a_i \notin \mathcal{T}} [-q^{(t)}_i + \lambda C(a_i, G)]$; \textbf{if} $V$ accepts the repaired $\tau$ \textbf{return} it.
    \State $\mathcal{T} \gets \mathcal{T} \cup \{a^\star\}$;\ \ update $q^{(t+1)}$ via Eq.~\ref{eq:spr-update}.
  \EndFor
\end{algorithmic}\end{algorithm}

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Tasks.} We evaluate \method on four canonical benchmarks spanning adversarial truthfulness, two-hop factual reasoning, three-hop reasoning, and long-context conversational recall, plus a native planning benchmark in Appendix~\ref{app:travel}. Datasets are loaded from HuggingFace with unmodified questions, contexts, and gold labels; the only modification is the injection of a synthetic user profile drawn from a bank of 28 realistic constraint templates (dietary, language, accessibility, expertise, budget). Profile assignment is deterministic per (\texttt{task\_id}, seed). Half the injected constraints are \emph{relevant} (must be respected); half are \emph{irrelevant distractors} (must not bias the factual answer). \textbf{Profile-injected TruthfulQA}~\cite{lin2021truthfulqa}: 817 imitative-falsehood questions; we sample $n{=}60$ per cell across three dataset seeds, with $n{=}36$ paired (task, seed) observations per cell after partial completion under provider rate limits. \textbf{Profile-injected 2WikiMultiHopQA}~\cite{ho2020twowiki}: $n{=}60$ two-hop questions per cell, single dataset seed. \textbf{Profile-injected LongMemEval}~\cite{wu2024longmemeval}: $n{=}60$ oracle-mode questions per cell over long conversational histories. \textbf{Profile-injected MuSiQue}~\cite{trivedi2022musique}: $n{=}60$ three-hop questions per cell.

\paragraph{Models.} Four LLMs across three training families and a 7--70\,B parameter range: Llama 3.1 8\,B Instruct~\cite{dubey2024llama3}, Mistral Nemo 12\,B Instruct~\cite{jiang2023mistral}, Qwen 2.5 7\,B Instruct~\cite{yang2024qwen2.5} via OpenRouter; Llama 3.3 70\,B Instruct~\cite{dubey2024llama3} via Together AI.

\paragraph{Baselines and protocol.} We compare \method against seven baselines on every (dataset, model) cell with an identical agent prompt; only the post-generation correction pipeline differs (Table~\ref{tab:methods}). For each (dataset, model, method) cell we sample 60 examples deterministically using $\texttt{Random}(42 \times 1{,}000{,}037 + \texttt{idx})$; the same example IDs and profiles are used across all methods, and we verify pairing correctness with 28 automated unit tests before committing results. A trial is successful iff both: (i) a \emph{factual check} (substring match of the gold answer for QA tasks, predicate satisfaction for planning) and (ii) a \emph{profile-violation check} (deterministic predicates plus calibrated LLM verification for semantic cases) pass; this benchmark scorer is used only for reporting and is distinct from the reference-free inference-time $S$ in Eq.~\ref{eq:repair-evidence}. Stochastic methods (Self-Refine, Reflexion, Full-Regen, ToT, \method) use three method-internal seeds $\{0,1,2\}$; TruthfulQA additionally varies three dataset seeds. For each (model, benchmark) cell we report a paired-bootstrap 95\% CI (10{,}000 resamples) for $\Delta = \text{IS} - \text{baseline}$ and McNemar's exact test on the paired $2{\times}2$ contingency table; $p$-values are Holm-Bonferroni-corrected at $\alpha{=}0.05$ within each pre-registered family (\method vs.\ Direct; \method vs.\ Reflexion). All \method hyperparameters are frozen on Profile-X validation splits with no per-cell tuning (Appendix~\ref{app:hparams}).

\paragraph{Pooled performance and regime structure.} Pooled across 864 paired observations (TruthfulQA contributes $4 \times 36 = 144$; the other three benchmarks $4 \times 60 = 240$ each), \method reaches \textbf{87.0\%}, exceeding Reflexion (85.4\%) by 1.6\,pp; single-round attribution alone yields 84.3\%, and SPR (Eq.~\ref{eq:spr-update}, Table~\ref{tab:spr-impact}) lifts this to 87.0\% by reallocating posterior mass to non-MAP ancestors after the first-round repair is rejected by $V$. The 70\,B regime is the cell pattern where the first-round MAP candidate is more often wrong (extracted ancestor sets are larger; the single-trial counterfactual is a noisier signal) but the correct ancestor remains inside $\mathcal{C}(v;G)$, which is exactly the regime SPR is designed for; the residual NS gap reflects cases where the true fault lies outside the extracted DAG. The 70\,B regime is represented by a single model family ($n{=}1$).


\subsection{Mechanism Diagnostics}

On synthetic \dagg tasks with known fault nodes ($n{=}60$), \method achieves 100\% top-1 attribution (MRR${=}1.0$) and 100\% repair success in \texttt{single\_fault} mode. Forward-only baselines score 0\% on this diagnostic. In \texttt{multi\_valid} mode, \method selects the minimum-cost valid repair on every trial.

\textbf{Single-round attribution components (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}).} Three components are load-bearing on the cells where the first-round repair is the deciding stage: removing the \dagg drops mean accuracy by 2.8\,pp ($-8.3$\,pp on TruthfulQA$\times$Mistral); replacing provenance-weighted priors with uniform priors drops 3.1\,pp ($-8.3$\,pp on TruthfulQA$\times$Qwen); skipping the counterfactual likelihood drops 1.5\,pp on average but 8.3\,pp on the most affected cell.

\textbf{Sequential Posterior Refinement (Appendix~\ref{app:spr-impl}, Table~\ref{tab:spr-impact}).} On the six cells where the verifier rejects the first-round repair, SPR contributes between $+3.3$ and $+11.7$\,pp ($+7.2$\,pp on average); pooled, this lifts \method from 84.3\% to 87.0\%. SPR derives its lift entirely from re-using the same posterior framework conditioned on the failure of the first repair (Eq.~\ref{eq:spr-update})---not from a different self-correction method---and is the methodological contrast with Reflexion (verbal critiques) vs.\ SPR (counterfactual evidence inside the same DAG-grounded posterior).

Two further findings warrant brief comment. First, increasing $M$ from 1 to 3 \emph{degrades} mean accuracy by 5.0\,pp; the full $M{\in}\{1,3,5\}$ sweep is non-monotone with a recovery at $M{=}5$ on one cell (Appendix~\ref{app:m-sweep}). The pattern is incompatible with an i.i.d.\ Bernoulli model of $r_{i,j}$ (which predicts monotone variance reduction $\propto 1/M$); we attribute it to within-candidate trial correlation (shared prompt context, shared decoding trace), so $M{=}1$ is the empirical operating point of the framework given the LLM generator's actual non-i.i.d.\ behaviour, not a statistically optimal estimator. Variance-reduced or seed-diversified counterfactual sampling are the right next steps to enable $M{>}1$ to provide net benefit. Second, the cost-aware repair objective contributes only $0.6$\,pp to raw accuracy; its operational value lies in bounded edit cost (\S\ref{sec:cost}), not in higher success rate.

\subsection{Is the Assumption-DAG Necessary? Violation-Aware Reprompt}\label{sec:vrp}

A natural alternative to the Assumption-DAG is to feed the verifier's violated profile span directly back into a re-prompt and let the agent revise its trajectory holistically, without any structured attribution. This isolates the contribution of the DAG from the contribution of \emph{having access to violation evidence at all}. We implement this as the \emph{violation-aware reprompt} (VRP) baseline: given a profile violation, format the violated profile span(s) as explicit feedback and re-prompt the agent for a single revised trajectory (one round, comparable cost to Self-Refine). Table~\ref{tab:vrp} compares VRP to \method on the four ablation cells.

On 2Wiki (saturation ceiling) and LongMemEval (where the prior already strongly discriminates), VRP and \method are statistically tied, consistent with the failure-taxonomy observation that the DAG matters most when the prior is uncertain (\S\ref{sec:ablations}).

\paragraph{Scope.} VRP is single-round; \method is multi-round. Table~\ref{tab:vrp} therefore establishes that \emph{violation evidence alone, applied naively, does not substitute for typed attribution}---it does not by itself isolate the DAG contribution from the iterative repair budget. A DAG-free baseline matching \method's retry budget over flat text (``Iter-VRP'') is the cleanest follow-up ablation; we did not run it for this submission.

\subsection{Failure-Mode Taxonomy on Ablation Cells}\label{sec:failure-taxonomy}

We categorize \method failures on the four ablation cells (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}) into five buckets: (i)~verifier miss, (ii)~wrong graph edge, (iii)~wrong prior, (iv)~counterfactual repair failure, and (v)~no valid downstream trajectory after repair. On these four cells, 100\% of \method failures occur at the repair stage (buckets iv--v): the verifier detects all violations and the attribution score correctly identifies the faulty node, but the repair itself either fails to remove the violation or introduces a new one during re-execution. We do not extend this analysis to the remaining twelve cells; the 100\% repair-stage statistic should be read as a property of the ablation cells, not as a global claim. The pattern motivates provenance-conditioned re-execution and diversified counterfactual sampling as the highest-leverage improvement directions on cells where attribution succeeds but repair fails.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Self-correction and search.} Self-Refine~\cite{madaan2023selfrefine}, Reflexion~\cite{shinn2023reflexion}, and self-verification~\cite{weng2023selfverif} retry forward by critiquing or reflecting on a failed trajectory; LATS~\cite{zhou2024lats}, ExpeL~\cite{zhao2024expel}, and AdaPlanner~\cite{sun2023adaplanner} extend this with search and learned reflection. Tree-of-Thoughts~\cite{yao2023tot}, Graph-of-Thoughts~\cite{besta2024got}, and RAP~\cite{hao2023rap} search over candidate completions. None of these localize \emph{which} latent assumption to revise; \method searches over candidate faulty assumptions rather than candidate completions.

\paragraph{Step-level error detection and process rewards.} CRITIC~\cite{gou2024critic} requires tool grounding; CoVe~\cite{dhuliawala2024cove} and SelfCheckGPT~\cite{manakul2023selfcheckgpt} detect hallucinations via consistency; process-level reward methods~\cite{lightman2023verify, wang2023mathshepherd} flag low-confidence steps but do not specify which user assumption to revise. Tyen et al.~\cite{tyen2024selfcorrect, tyen2024llmerrors} establish the gap \method targets: LLMs detect errors but consistently fail at \emph{localizing} them.

\paragraph{Trajectory-grounded fault attribution.} RAFFLES~\cite{tyen2024raffles} introduces a Judge-LLM with iterative fault localization; ContextWeaver~\cite{liu2024contextweaver} organizes interaction history as a dependency graph; Theorem-of-Thought~\cite{peng2024theoremofthought} performs Bayesian belief propagation over reasoning graphs; StateAct~\cite{rozanov2025stateact} tracks chain-of-states forward at every step. \method is complementary: it focuses on profile-grounded violations specifically, treats profile-derived assumptions as immutable, and couples backward attribution to selective downstream repair rather than forward state tracking.

\paragraph{Personalization and constraint-aware planning.} \method targets the trajectory-level version of the personalization-induced hallucination problem~\cite{sun2024pfqabench, xie2024travelplanner}; LaMP~\cite{salemi2023lamp}, TravelAgent~\cite{chen2024travelagent}, and MemoryBank~\cite{zhong2024memorybank} provide complementary personalization frameworks. On native multi-constraint planning, specialized architectures (ATLAS~\cite{choi2025atlas}, Behavior Forest~\cite{yuan2026behaviorforest}, TriFlow~\cite{chen2026triflow}) substantially outperform general-purpose self-correction; \method does not compete in that regime (Appendix~\ref{app:travel}).

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

At $n{=}60$ per cell ($n{=}36$ for TruthfulQA after partial completion), the per-cell paired-McNemar MDE at $\alpha{=}0.05$ is approximately 13\,pp; per-cell claims in this paper are therefore descriptive, with directional accuracy claims supported only at the pooled level (864 paired observations). The 100\% repair-stage failure statistic in §\ref{sec:failure-taxonomy} is scoped to the four ablation cells only. DAG extraction precision/recall are measured on synthetic diagnostics (Appendix~\ref{app:dag-quality}) only; human-annotated fault labels on natural trajectories are the highest-priority follow-up. Profile-injected variants of public benchmarks shift the construct from pure factuality to factuality-plus-profile-following, controlled within a row by paired sampling; native user-conditioned benchmarks (Appendix~\ref{app:travel}) provide a complementary evaluation track but do not reproduce the gains seen on Profile-injected QA. The relationship to Reflexion is conceptually parallel and mechanistically distinct: SPR conditions on the same DAG-grounded posterior $q$ as round one (Eq.~\ref{eq:spr-update}) and re-targets the next-best ancestor's downstream subgraph---no verbal critique, no full re-generation, no Reflexion subroutine call. The single NS loss (LongMemEval$\times$Llama 70\,B, $-1.7$\,pp) is consistent with this characterization: it is a regime where the true fault frequently lies outside the extracted ancestor set, so neither round of SPR can reach it while Reflexion's free-form regeneration sometimes can. The cleaner ablation isolating the DAG (an iterative-VRP baseline matching \method's retry budget over flat text) requires re-executing every cell under a new method definition and is the highest-priority follow-up ablation we did not run.

\section*{Broader Impact}\label{sec:broader-impact}

\method makes latent agent assumptions explicit and auditable, which improves safety for user-facing deployments by exposing the inferred user attributes that drove a decision. The same representation creates a privacy surface: an \dagg materializes inferred user attributes as structured text that downstream systems may retain. Deployments should minimize retained graph state, separate profile-derived facts from model-inferred assumptions, and expose assumption logs to users for correction.

\section{Conclusion}

Across sixteen (dataset, model) cells, \method achieves the best or tied-best task success on fifteen with no significant losses, reaches a pooled mean of 87.0\% that exceeds the strongest baseline (Reflexion, 85.4\%) by 1.6\,pp, and matches or exceeds Tree-of-Thoughts accuracy at $11.8$--$14.9\times$ lower token cost. The contribution is a unified pipeline of typed counterfactual fault attribution and selective subgraph repair over an Assumption-DAG, with Sequential Posterior Refinement (Eq.~\ref{eq:spr-update}) reusing the same posterior, prior, and re-execution prompt to close the residual gap when the first-round MAP candidate misidentifies the fault.

\section{Algorithm and SPR Implementation Details}\label{app:spr-impl}

\paragraph{Implementation notes.} During counterfactual trials (Algorithm~\ref{alg:intro-specter}, Line~8), the repaired trajectories are cached; the final re-execution (Line~13) reuses the cached trajectory of $a^\star$ rather than regenerating it. The worked example in Figure~\ref{fig:worked-example} depicts a multi-fault scenario; in the current implementation this is handled by a sequential single-node pass: after repairing $a^\star$, re-verification may detect a residual violation triggering a second single-node repair. All hyperparameters ($M$, provenance priors, cost weights, $\delta$, $\lambda$, $T_{\text{spr}}$, $\alpha$) are fixed on Profile-X development splits before any Profile-injected-X evaluation; values are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:hparams}. The decay $\alpha$ encodes our credence that a verifier rejection is partial rather than conclusive evidence: $\alpha{\to}1$ recovers the prior (no learning from failure), $\alpha{\to}0$ recovers hard exclusion (which over-commits to ``wrong fault'' over the alternative ``right fault, bad repair''). At $T_{\text{spr}} = 2$ and our cell sizes, the second-round selector is essentially insensitive to $\alpha \in [0, 0.3]$ once the rejected candidate's mass is reduced by $10\times$ or more. The routing signal (``did $V$ accept the repaired output?'') uses no gold labels; SPR addresses what we call the \emph{single-handed} regime---failures where the first-round MAP candidate misidentifies the fault but the true fault is still inside the original ancestor set $\mathcal{C}(v;G)$.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{\method (full)}\label{alg:intro-specter}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \Require task $x$, profile $P$, trajectory $\tau$, verifier $V$, success evaluator $S$, threshold $\delta$, SPR rounds $T_{\text{spr}}$
  \Ensure  accepted, repaired, or abstained trajectory
  \State Construct \dagg $G = (A, E)$ from $\tau$.
  \State Run verifier $V(P, x, \tau, y)$.
  \If{no violation} \Return $\tau$ \EndIf
  \State $\mathcal{C}(v; G) \gets$ ancestors of violated step in $G$, excluding profile-derived nodes.
  \For{each $a_i \in \mathcal{C}(v; G)$}
    \State Sample $M$ counterfactual repairs of $a_i$.
    \State Re-execute downstream subgraph $D_i$ (cache trajectory).
    \State Compute $r_i$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:repair-evidence}), $q_i$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:attribution}).
  \EndFor
  \If{$\max_i q_i < \delta$} \Return abstain or full re-generation. \EndIf
  \State $\mathcal{T} \gets \emptyset$;\quad $q^{(0)} \gets q$.
  \For{$t = 0, 1, \ldots, T_{\text{spr}}$}
    \State Select $a^\star \gets \arg\min_{a_i \notin \mathcal{T}} \bigl[-q^{(t)}_i + \lambda\, C(a_i, G)\bigr]$;\quad $\tau^{\text{repair}} \gets$ cached trajectory for $a^\star$.
    \State Verify $\tau^{\text{repair}}$ via $V$; \textbf{if} verifier passes \textbf{then} \Return $\tau^{\text{repair}}$.
    \State $\mathcal{T} \gets \mathcal{T} \cup \{a^\star\}$;\quad update $q^{(t+1)}$ via Eq.~\ref{eq:spr-update}.
    \If{$\mathcal{T} = \mathcal{C}(v;G)$ \textbf{or} $t = T_{\text{spr}}$} \textbf{break} \EndIf
  \EndFor
  \State \Return $\tau^{\text{repair}}$ from the final round (verifier-rejected; reported as repair\_failed).
\end{algorithmic}\end{algorithm}

\section{Reproducibility Details}\label{app:repro}

\paragraph{Inference infrastructure.} All LLM calls are issued via remote provider APIs, not local GPU inference: Llama 3.3 70\,B Instruct through Together AI's hosted inference; Llama 3.1 8\,B Instruct, Mistral Nemo 12\,B Instruct, and Qwen 2.5 7\,B Instruct through OpenRouter's hosted inference. We do not maintain a local GPU cluster; the only local compute used is a single laptop (Apple Silicon) for orchestration and metric aggregation. Total API spend across all 16 cells, the synthetic diagnostics, the ablations, and the SPR overlay reruns: approximately USD 70 (cumulative across the run window of March--May 2026). The orchestration laptop ran Python 3.11 in a virtual environment; pinned package versions are in \texttt{requirements.txt} in the released code.

\paragraph{Caching and determinism.} All model calls are cached by SHA-256 hash of \texttt{(provider, model, temperature, system\_prompt, user\_prompt, max\_tokens, seed)}. Cache hits return identical bytes; the same (\texttt{task\_id}, seed) pair therefore yields identical method outputs across reruns. Cache database is shipped as a single SQLite file alongside the released code, so an external reviewer can re-execute the metric pipeline end-to-end without paying any API cost.

\paragraph{Seeds.} Example sampling uses seed 42 (deterministic hash-based index $\texttt{Random}(42 \times 1{,}000{,}037 + \texttt{idx})$). Method-internal stochasticity (Self-Refine, Reflexion, Full-Regen, ToT, \method) uses three method seeds $\{0, 1, 2\}$; reported success rates average across seeds. TruthfulQA uses three additional dataset seeds; 2Wiki, LongMemEval, and MuSiQue use a single dataset seed.

\paragraph{Inference parameters.} Temperature is 0.0 for deterministic baselines (Direct, ReAct) and for the \method extraction, verifier, counterfactual, and re-execution prompts; 0.7 for stochastic-by-design baselines (Self-Refine, Reflexion, Full-Regen, ToT). Max-tokens is 8192 for the agent prompt and trajectory regeneration calls (raised from 4096 after we observed truncation on long ALFWorld and TravelPlanner plans), 2048 for the verifier and counterfactual prompts. Default top-p is 1.0; we do not modify it.

\paragraph{Run window and orchestration.} All evaluation runs occurred between March and May 2026 against the provider models available at that time. Each cell is run by a single \texttt{run\_real\_benchmark.py} invocation that reads a YAML config (\texttt{configs/real/}), iterates over methods and seeds, and writes one JSONL row per task to \texttt{outputs/real/<dataset>\_\_<model>/}. Per-cell aggregation (\texttt{scripts/aggregate\_real\_benchmarks.py}) and p-value regeneration (\texttt{scripts/regen\_pvalue\_tables.py}) consume those JSONLs to produce the tables in this paper.

\paragraph{Repository contents.} The released code includes: full prompts (Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}), all benchmark loaders, all baseline implementations, the SPR pipeline, the verifier, the cost model, the SQLite cache file, all 16 cell YAML configs, the aggregation and regeneration scripts, the per-cell JSONL outputs that produced the headline tables, and a single notebook reproducing every figure and table in this paper.

\section{Prompts}\label{app:prompts}

Four prompts drive \method's pipeline. We list each system prompt verbatim below; the user-prompt formatters are deterministic JSON-dump wrappers around the inputs and are reproduced in the released code.

\paragraph{1. Assumption extraction.} System prompt invoked once per trajectory to construct the Assumption-DAG (\S\ref{sec:method}).

{\small\begin{verbatim}
You are extracting explicit assumptions from an agent trajectory for evaluation.

Inputs:
- User profile JSON
- Task
- Agent trajectory with steps, actions, observations, and final output

Return JSON only with:
- nodes: assumption nodes used by the agent
- edges: dependencies among assumptions
- final_decision_node: the node most directly supporting the final output

For each node, include:
- id (string, e.g. "a0", "a1", ...)
- step_id (integer, matches a trajectory step_id)
- assumption: one sentence
- provenance: one of profile, tool, external_evidence, model_inferred,
  world_knowledge
- profile_span_ids: list of profile evidence spans, or empty list
- confidence: number from 0 to 1
- depends_on: list of node ids

For each edge, include:
- source: assumption id
- target: assumption id
- type: one of supports, depends_on, contradicts, downstream_of

Do not include hidden chain-of-thought. Use concise observable reasoning
summaries only.
Output a single valid JSON object and nothing else.
\end{verbatim}}

\paragraph{2. Profile-grounded verification.} System prompt invoked to detect violations against the profile (\S\ref{sec:verifier}).

{\small\begin{verbatim}
You are a profile-grounded verifier.

Inputs:
- User profile JSON with span ids
- Task
- Agent trajectory
- Final output

Return JSON only:
{
  "pass": true or false,
  "violations": [
    {
      "violation_id": "v1",
      "step_id": integer,
      "violated_profile_span_id": "p3",
      "violated_constraint": "string",
      "trajectory_text": "string",
      "severity": "low|medium|high",
      "confidence": number from 0 to 1,
      "explanation": "one sentence"
    }
  ]
}

A violation exists if the trajectory or final output contradicts an explicit
profile fact, depends on an unsupported inferred user property, or allows
irrelevant profile context to distort a factual answer.

Output a single valid JSON object and nothing else.
\end{verbatim}}

\paragraph{3. Counterfactual repair generation.} System prompt invoked once per candidate ancestor to test whether a minimal change at that node removes the violation (\S\ref{sec:attribution}).

{\small\begin{verbatim}
You are testing whether an assumption is the root cause of a profile-grounded
error.

Inputs:
- User profile JSON
- Task
- Current Assumption-DAG
- Candidate faulty node
- Original trajectory
- Detected violation

Generate 3 counterfactual repairs that minimally change the candidate node
while preserving upstream assumptions. Each repair should describe what the
new assumption is, which downstream nodes need to be re-run, and whether the
violation is expected to be removed.

Return JSON only:
{
  "candidate_node_id": "a4",
  "repairs": [
    {
      "repair_id": "r1",
      "new_assumption": "string",
      "nodes_to_rerun": ["a4", "a5", "a6"],
      "repair_instruction": "string",
      "expected_violation_removed": true,
      "risk_notes": "string"
    }
  ]
}

Output a single valid JSON object and nothing else.
\end{verbatim}}

\paragraph{4. Downstream re-execution.} System prompt invoked once per selected repair candidate (round one and each SPR round) to regenerate only the descendants of the repaired node (\S\ref{sec:mcgr}).

{\small\begin{verbatim}
You are repairing only the downstream part of an agent trajectory.

Inputs:
- User profile JSON
- Task
- Original valid prefix steps
- Repaired assumption node
- Downstream nodes to regenerate
- Tool observations available so far

Regenerate the downstream trajectory from the repaired assumption onward.

Constraints:
- Preserve all valid prefix steps.
- Do not invent new user preferences.
- If a profile fact is missing, mark it unknown instead of assuming it.
- Use available tool observations exactly as given.
- Return structured JSON with repaired steps and final output.

Return JSON only with the schema:
{
  "repaired_steps": [{"step_id": int, "kind": "...", "text": "...",
                      "reason_summary": "..."}],
  "final_output": "string"
}

Output a single valid JSON object and nothing else.
\end{verbatim}}

User-prompt formatters for all four prompts (the deterministic JSON-dump wrappers around inputs) and the baseline-method prompts (Self-Refine critique, Reflexion reflect/retry, ReAct, Detection-Only, ToT generate/evaluate/solve, SelfCheckGPT sample/consistency/regenerate, Direct agent) are reproduced verbatim in \texttt{intro\_specter/prompts.py} in the released code.

\section{Synthetic Benchmark Details}\label{app:synthetic}
The synthetic \dagg generator constructs random DAGs of 8--15 nodes by sampling node provenances from the empirical mix observed in development trajectories, sampling edges to satisfy a target average in-degree of 2.0, and injecting one or more known faulty assumptions whose downstream effects propagate to a deterministic verifier. The verifier checks whether the trajectory output satisfies a set of compositional constraints derived from the gold graph. We generate 60 such graphs per condition. In \texttt{single\_fault}, exactly one ancestor is faulty; in \texttt{multi\_valid}, multiple ancestors can each independently resolve the violation but at different downstream edit costs.

\paragraph{Calibration to the official TravelPlanner evaluator.} We caveat the absolute numbers above. Our success criterion is a permissive subset of the official TravelPlanner evaluator: we apply only the dataset's hard-constraint predicates (budget, allergies, transportation rules, room counts, and accommodation/cuisine includes) plus our injected profile-violation predicates. We do \emph{not} apply the official evaluator's commonsense-constraint pass (e.g., realistic transit times, consistent attraction-to-attraction scheduling, no overnight transit between cities) nor the environmental-constraint pass (validity of the returned restaurants, attractions, and accommodations against the dataset's reference databases). The official evaluator's \emph{Final Pass Rate} requires all three layers (hard + commonsense + environmental) to pass simultaneously; under that stricter rubric, recent specialized planners (ATLAS~\cite{choi2025atlas}: 44.4\%, Behavior Forest~\cite{yuan2026behaviorforest}: up to 94.4\%, TriFlow~\cite{chen2026triflow}: 91.1\%) substantially outperform any general-purpose self-correction baseline including \method.

The 80.6\% Reflexion rate on Llama 8\,B in our table should not be read as a state-of-the-art TravelPlanner result---it reflects only that, on the subset of constraints we score, Reflexion's full-trajectory regeneration tends to satisfy the explicit hard constraints we retained. This appendix is included to complete the per-method comparison on user-conditioned benchmarks, not to claim a TravelPlanner accuracy result. The relevant comparison is the within-row $\Delta$ between methods evaluated on the identical predicate set; the absolute level is uncalibrated against the official benchmark.

The mean trend is decreasing in $M$ but the per-cell pattern is non-monotone, which is the diagnostic signal we discuss in §\ref{sec:ablations}. Per-cell behavior: TruthfulQA $\times$ Mistral shows a monotone decrease (86.1 $\to$ 80.6 $\to$ 72.2); TruthfulQA $\times$ Qwen drops at $M{=}3$ then plateaus; 2Wiki $\times$ Mistral is at the saturation ceiling and does not move; \textbf{LongMemEval $\times$ Llama 8B drops at $M{=}3$ then recovers to the $M{=}1$ value at $M{=}5$}. The recovery on LongMemEval is the diagnostic that rules out a simple i.i.d.\ Bernoulli interpretation: under that model, $\hat{r}_i$ would have variance $\theta_i(1-\theta_i)/M$, monotone-decreasing in $M$, and accuracy should improve (or at minimum not recover non-monotonically) as $M$ grows. The pattern is more consistent with the within-candidate trials being correlated rather than independent (shared prompt context, shared decoding trace), so larger $M$ does not realize the variance reduction the i.i.d.\ model would predict and instead introduces small misranking events on cells with closely matched priors (TruthfulQA), while having negligible net effect on cells where the prior already strongly discriminates (2Wiki and LongMemEval). Variance-reduced or seed-diversified counterfactual sampling are the natural next steps; we use $M{=}1$ as the empirical operating point of the current framework.
