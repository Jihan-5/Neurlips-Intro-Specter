\begin{abstract}
Self-correction lets an LLM agent recover a failed trajectory without retraining, but existing methods retry the task, search alternative reasoning paths, or re-prompt against the violated constraint, and fault-attribution methods localize failures to steps or agents without modeling the assumptions that caused them or repairing the trajectory. We study trajectories conditioned on a structured profile of user constraints, where a single incorrect assumption adopted early propagates silently and surfaces only as a terminal violation. \method extracts the assumptions a trajectory relied on, organizes them into a provenance-aware dependency graph, isolates the root-cause assumption by counterfactual intervention, and regenerates only the dependent suffix. On injected faults it recovers the true root cause in 100\% of trajectories (top-1, MRR${=}1.0$). In single-fault settings it maintains parity with the strongest baselines (89.4\% pooled over 648 paired observations). Across five benchmarks, four LLMs, and ${\sim}81{,}000$ trajectories carrying two to five simultaneous faults, it outperforms six baselines on every dataset, with margins that grow with fault count (up to $+17.3$\,pp over the strongest baseline on HotpotQA at four concurrent faults). Under 100 seeded re-draws of every user profile (40{,}800 paired outcomes), its advantage over Reflexion is $+2.1$\,pp and favorable in 95 of 100 draws. On real, non-injected user histories (PersonalWAB), it exceeds direct prompting by $+8.1$\,pp ($p<10^{-4}$) and iterative reprompting baselines by $+3.3$--$4.4$\,pp ($p\le0.03$), and is statistically indistinguishable from Reflexion ($-0.8$\,pp, $p=0.61$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

LLMs~\cite{ouyang2022instructgpt, openai2023gpt4} deployed as user-facing agents operate over a structured profile of preferences, constraints, and user-specific facts. Because users rarely inspect every intermediate step, a mistake made early in the trajectory propagates silently until it surfaces as a final violation: a CRM copilot acting on a stale client record, a healthcare intake agent relying on an outdated medication or allergy list, a recommendation that treats an inferred preference as explicit. The same failure can arise when a finance assistant uses stale risk tolerance or a coding agent follows an outdated repository configuration. We call these \emph{user-specific hallucinations}: outputs that may be internally coherent but violate, overgeneralize, or contradict the user's own stated constraints~\cite{sun2024pfqabench, xie2024travelplanner}. Existing self-correction methods do not directly target this failure mode---Self-Refine~\cite{madaan2023selfrefine} and Reflexion~\cite{shinn2023reflexion} retry forward, Tree-of-Thoughts~\cite{yao2023tot} and Graph-of-Thoughts~\cite{besta2024got} search over completions, and process-level reward methods~\cite{lightman2023verify} flag low-confidence steps but do not localize which latent user assumption to revise. Tyen et al.~\cite{tyen2024llmerrors} make this gap concrete: LLMs can detect errors but consistently fail at \emph{localizing} which step is wrong, and He et al.~\cite{he2025selfcorrect} argue that self-correction must be evaluated as more than output refinement.

We introduce \method, a framework for counterfactual fault attribution in user-conditioned agent trajectories. \method represents a trajectory as a provenance-typed Assumption-DAG (profile, tool, model-inferred, world) over observable trajectory artifacts. When a profile-grounded violation is detected, the system estimates an attribution score over candidate faulty ancestors using counterfactual repair trials and selects a repair point by trading off fault probability against downstream edit cost; if the post-repair verifier still rejects the trajectory, Sequential Posterior Refinement (SPR; Eq.~\ref{eq:spr-update}) treats that rejection as additional counterfactual evidence and updates the same posterior in closed form, retargeting the next-best ancestor without invoking any auxiliary mechanism. None of our components is new in isolation: abductive diagnosis, dependency graphs, and counterfactual intervention each have a multi-decade history in model-based diagnosis and software fault localization (\S\ref{sec:related}). The contribution is the setting-driven construction that makes those classical principles applicable to a single stochastic agent trajectory---and the closed loop they form once applied. Concretely: (i) \textbf{a construction that transfers classical abductive diagnosis to LLM agent trajectories by synthesizing the execution spectrum classical methods presuppose}---fault localization \`a la Tarantula/Ochiai requires many executions with pass/fail labels over shared components, while a trajectory supplies one execution and one failure; our counterfactual repair trials manufacture that missing spectrum (Eq.~\ref{eq:repair-evidence}), over a component set (the Assumption-DAG) that is itself \emph{inferred} rather than given---a failure mode no classical setting faces, which is why extraction quality is a first-class metric here (Appendix~\ref{app:dag-quality}); (ii) \textbf{the closed loop in which repair execution is the diagnostic evidence}: the likelihood proxy \emph{is} the repair attempt (Eq.~\ref{eq:repair-evidence}), that score selects the repair point against downstream edit cost (Eq.~\ref{eq:mcgr}), and verifier rejection re-enters as evidence on the same posterior (Eq.~\ref{eq:spr-update}); no prior fault-attribution system closes this loop---localization elsewhere terminates in a judgment---and our ablations show the loop is load-bearing, not compositional (removing the DAG costs 2.8\,pp, uniform priors 3.1\,pp, and disabling SPR drops pooled success below the Reflexion baseline; \S\ref{sec:ablations}); (iii) \textbf{a falsifiable operating envelope}: attribution requires at least one uncorrupted anchor---a clean evidence span, untouched constraint, or genuine user history---against which a counterfactual repair can be validated; our saturation experiments confirm the boundary in both directions (\S\ref{sec:multifault}, \S\ref{sec:limitations}); and (iv) \textbf{a paired evaluation} across benchmarks and LLMs, including real user histories (\S\ref{sec:personalwab}) and multi-fault stress tests (\S\ref{sec:multifault}), with losses and saturation regimes reported alongside wins.

\section{\method}\label{sec:method}

Let $x$ denote a user task and $P = \{p_1, \ldots, p_m\}$ a structured user profile. An agent produces a trajectory $\tau = (s_1, u_1, o_1, \ldots, s_T, u_T, o_T, y)$ with steps $s_t$, actions or tool calls $u_t$, observations $o_t$, and final output $y$. A profile-grounded verifier $V(P, x, \tau, y) \in \{0,1\}$ returns 1 when the trajectory or output violates the profile (a \emph{profile-grounded violation}); a reference-free task-success evaluator $S(x, \tau, y) \in \{0,1\}$ judges task-appropriateness from observable trajectory artifacts without access to ground-truth labels (an LLM-based coherence and constraint-satisfaction check, not the gold-answer substring matcher used for benchmark scoring; \S\ref{sec:experiments}). A profile-grounded violation is a \emph{user-specific hallucination} when at least one assumption used in producing $\tau$ or $y$ is unsupported by, contradicted by, or improperly generalized from $P$.

\paragraph{Assumption-DAG.} \method represents the latent commitments of a trajectory as a directed acyclic graph $G = (A, E)$, where each node $a_i \in A$ is an assumption $a_i = (c_i, \rho_i, t_i, \ell_i)$ with one-sentence commitment $c_i$, provenance $\rho_i \in \{\text{profile}, \text{tool}, \text{model}, \text{world}\}$, trajectory step $t_i$, and confidence $\ell_i \in [0,1]$; edges encode dependency. An LLM-based extractor is prompted with $(P, x, \tau)$ and returns JSON containing each assumption's commitment, step, provenance, profile evidence spans, confidence, and dependency list. Edges are added when one assumption justifies, specializes, or enables another. Cycles, when detected, are resolved by removing the edge whose source node has the lowest confidence; the cycle-removal rate is logged. Extraction precision and recall on synthetic diagnostics (where the gold graph is known by construction) are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:dag-quality}; full prompt in Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}.

\paragraph{Profile-grounded verification.}\label{sec:verifier} The verifier $V$ combines deterministic predicates (budget, allergy, date, location, forbidden-action) with an LLM-based pass for semantic cases not covered by rules; LLM outputs are calibrated against the rule-based layer on a held-out development split. Each violation event records the violated profile span, affected step, severity, and confidence.

\paragraph{Counterfactual fault attribution.}\label{sec:attribution} Given a violation $v$ at trajectory step $t_v$, the goal is to infer which earlier assumption caused it. Let $F \in A$ denote the latent faulty assumption. Because the true likelihood $p(v \mid F{=}a_i, G, \tau, P)$ is unavailable, \method uses counterfactual repair success as empirical evidence for fault assignment. Let $\mathcal{C}(v; G) = \{a_i \in A \mid t_i \leq t_v, a_i \in \Anc(v;G)\}$ denote the candidate set: extracted ancestors of the violation step, including profile-provenance ancestors. Repairing a profile-provenance candidate revises its downstream use while leaving the stored user profile unchanged. For each candidate $a_i \in \mathcal{C}(v; G)$, the system samples $M$ minimal repairs and measures whether repairing $a_i$ removes the violation while preserving task success:
\begin{equation}
  r_i = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{j=1}^{M} \mathbf{1}[V(P,x,\tau^{(i,j)},y^{(i,j)})=0] \cdot \mathbf{1}[S(x,\tau^{(i,j)},y^{(i,j)})=1],\label{eq:repair-evidence}
\end{equation}
where $\tau^{(i,j)}$ is the trajectory obtained by repairing $a_i$ under sample $j$. The attribution score combines repair evidence with the provenance prior via prior-weighted normalization:
\begin{equation}
  q_i = \frac{\pi_{\rho_i}\, r_i}{\sum_{a_k \in \mathcal{C}(v;G)} \pi_{\rho_k}\, r_k},\label{eq:attribution}
\end{equation}
where $\pi_{\rho_i}$ is a provenance-dependent prior ($\pi_{\text{model}} > \pi_{\text{world}} > \pi_{\text{tool}}$; values in Table~\ref{tab:hparams}). When $\sum_k \pi_{\rho_k} r_k = 0$ (no counterfactual sample removed the violation), we fall back to prior-only attribution $q_i = \pi_{\rho_i} / \sum_k \pi_{\rho_k}$. We refer to $q_i$ as an \emph{attribution score} rather than a calibrated posterior: $r_i$ is a Monte Carlo estimate from intervention trials, not a calibrated likelihood, and $q$ is not calibrated to the true generative process.

\paragraph{Min-cost graph-edit repair.}\label{sec:mcgr}

For candidate $a_i$, let $D_i$ be the downstream subgraph and $C(a_i, G)$ the normalized edit cost:
\begin{equation}
  C(a_i, G) = w_n \frac{|D_i|}{|A|} + w_t \frac{\hat{T}_i}{T_{\max}} + w_c \frac{\hat{K}_i}{K_{\max}} + w_p \,\mathbf{1}[a_i \text{ precedes a verified tool observation}],\label{eq:edit-cost}
\end{equation}
where $|D_i|$ is the number of downstream assumptions to re-execute, $\hat{T}_i$ and $\hat{K}_i$ are estimated regenerated-token and re-issued-tool-call counts (derived by summing per-step token and tool-call counts of $D_i$ from the original trajectory), $T_{\max}$ and $K_{\max}$ are the original trajectory's total token and tool-call counts (used for normalization), and the final term penalizes edits that may invalidate already-verified tool observations. \method selects
\begin{equation}
  a^\star = \arg\min_{a_i \in \mathcal{C}(v;G)} \bigl[-q_i + \lambda\, C(a_i, G)\bigr],\label{eq:mcgr}
\end{equation}
abstaining or falling back to full regeneration when $\max_i q_i < \delta$. We call this the \emph{cost-regularized attribution objective}: it minimizes a regularized score under the approximate attribution score $q$ and cost $C$. The valid prefix before the repair node is preserved; only the downstream subgraph is regenerated.

\paragraph{Sequential Posterior Refinement (SPR).} When the post-repair verifier rejects $\tau^{\text{repair}}$, that rejection is partial evidence against $a^\star$: it is consistent with both ``wrong candidate'' and ``right candidate, bad repair''. Rather than permanently excluding tried candidates, we down-weight them by a Bayesian decay factor $\alpha \in [0,1)$ and renormalize:
\begin{equation}
  q^{(t+1)}_i = \begin{cases} \alpha \cdot q^{(t)}_i \big/ Z^{(t)} & a_i \in \mathcal{T}^{(t)} \\ q^{(t)}_i \big/ Z^{(t)} & \text{otherwise,} \end{cases} \quad Z^{(t)} = \alpha \!\!\sum_{a_j \in \mathcal{T}^{(t)}}\! q^{(t)}_j + \!\!\sum_{a_j \notin \mathcal{T}^{(t)}}\! q^{(t)}_j,\label{eq:spr-update}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{T}^{(t)}$ is the set of candidates whose repair $V$ has already rejected. We use $\alpha = 0.1$ throughout, frozen on the Profile-X dev split (Appendix~\ref{app:hparams}); the second-round selector is insensitive to $\alpha \in [0, 0.3]$ at our cell sizes. We then re-select $a^\star$ via Eq.~\ref{eq:mcgr} on $q^{(t+1)}$, re-execute, and re-verify; the loop runs for at most $T_{\text{spr}}$ rounds. SPR is strictly sequential abductive inference over the same Assumption-DAG and posterior framework as the first round---no auxiliary model, no verbal critique, no full re-generation. Disabling SPR ($T_{\text{spr}}=0$) reverts \method to the targeted-repair-only configuration (Appendix~\ref{app:spr-impl}, Table~\ref{tab:spr-impact}).

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{\method (sketch). Full pseudocode with caching, abstention, and SPR loop in Appendix~\ref{app:spr-impl}.}\label{alg:intro-specter-mini}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \Require task $x$, profile $P$, trajectory $\tau$, verifier $V$
  \State Build \dagg $G$; if $V$ accepts $\tau$ \textbf{return} $\tau$.
  \State $\mathcal{C}(v;G) \gets$ extracted ancestors of the violated step; score each via Eqs.~\ref{eq:repair-evidence}--\ref{eq:attribution}.
  \For{$t = 0, \ldots, T_{\text{spr}}$}
    \State $a^\star \gets \arg\min_{a_i \notin \mathcal{T}} [-q^{(t)}_i + \lambda C(a_i, G)]$; \textbf{if} $V$ accepts the repaired $\tau$ \textbf{return} it.
    \State $\mathcal{T} \gets \mathcal{T} \cup \{a^\star\}$;\ \ update $q^{(t+1)}$ via Eq.~\ref{eq:spr-update}.
  \EndFor
\end{algorithmic}\end{algorithm}

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Tasks.} We evaluate \method on four canonical benchmarks spanning adversarial truthfulness, two-hop factual reasoning, three-hop reasoning, and long-context conversational recall, plus a native planning benchmark in Appendix~\ref{app:travel}. Datasets are loaded from HuggingFace with unmodified questions, contexts, and gold labels; the only modification is the injection of a synthetic user profile drawn from a bank of 28 realistic constraint templates (dietary, language, accessibility, expertise, budget). Profile assignment is deterministic per (\texttt{task\_id}, seed). Half the injected constraints are \emph{relevant} (must be respected); half are \emph{irrelevant distractors} (must not bias the factual answer). \textbf{Profile-injected TruthfulQA}~\cite{lin2021truthfulqa}: 817 imitative-falsehood questions; the frozen loader selects 12 held-out questions and evaluates three configuration seeds, yielding $n{=}36$ paired observations per cell. \textbf{Profile-injected 2WikiMultiHopQA}~\cite{ho2020twowiki}: $n{=}60$ two-hop questions per cell, single configuration seed. \textbf{Profile-injected LongMemEval}~\cite{wu2024longmemeval}: $n{=}60$ oracle-mode questions per cell over long conversational histories. \textbf{Profile-injected MuSiQue}~\cite{trivedi2022musique}: $n{=}60$ three-hop questions per cell.

\paragraph{Models.} Four LLMs across three training families and a 7--70\,B parameter range: Llama 3.1 8\,B Instruct~\cite{dubey2024llama3}, Mistral Nemo 12\,B Instruct~\cite{jiang2023mistral}, Qwen 2.5 7\,B Instruct~\cite{yang2024qwen2.5} via OpenRouter; Llama 3.3 70\,B Instruct~\cite{dubey2024llama3} via Together AI.

\paragraph{Baselines and protocol.} We compare \method against seven baselines on every (dataset, model) cell with an identical agent prompt; only the post-generation correction pipeline differs (Table~\ref{tab:methods}). The same example IDs, profiles, and configuration seeds are paired across methods. A trial is successful iff both: (i) a \emph{factual check} (substring match of the gold answer for QA tasks, predicate satisfaction for planning) and (ii) a \emph{profile-violation check} (deterministic predicates plus calibrated LLM verification for semantic cases) pass; this benchmark scorer is used only for reporting and is distinct from the reference-free inference-time $S$ in Eq.~\ref{eq:repair-evidence}. TruthfulQA stores three configuration seeds per held-out question; the other headline cells store one. Each stored binary row is a paired observation, and no seed averaging precedes McNemar's exact test. For each (model, benchmark) cell we report a paired-bootstrap 95\% CI (10{,}000 resamples) for $\Delta = \text{IS} - \text{baseline}$ and McNemar's exact test on the paired $2{\times}2$ contingency table; Holm correction is applied within each baseline family across the selected cells. All \method hyperparameters are frozen on Profile-X validation splits with no per-cell tuning (Appendix~\ref{app:hparams}).

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Scope.} The primary single-fault matrix comprises the twelve (dataset, model) cells with SPR-enabled runs on the three runnable models (Llama 3.1 8\,B, Llama 3.3 70\,B, Mistral Nemo 12\,B); Qwen 2.5 7\,B became unavailable at every serving provider before the rerun, and its archival no-SPR results, together with three exploratory models, are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:archival-matrix} under a mixed-configuration label. Tables~\ref{tab:truthfulqa}--\ref{tab:musique} report per-cell success (\%); \textbf{bold} marks all tied numerical maxima. Paired-statistics tables are in Appendix~\ref{app:full-tables}.

\paragraph{Per-cell outcomes and pooled performance.} Across the twelve cells \method records 579/648 successes (\textbf{89.35\%}); against Reflexion (86.73\% on the same 648 matched observations) the difference is $+2.62$\,pp, with a cell record of 8 numerical wins, 2 ties, and 2 losses (LongMemEval and MuSiQue on Llama 70\,B). No individual cell survives Holm adjustment in either direction, so per-cell claims are descriptive. Two protocol caveats apply throughout: baseline rows are archival runs paired by (task, seed) keys, and pairing does not remove provider drift between run windows; missing observations are excluded, never scored as failures. Read as designed, the single-fault matrix is a parity-to-modest-lead result at matched cost---consistent with the mechanism: with exactly one injected fault and a strong retry baseline, whole-trajectory regeneration and targeted repair converge, and the regimes where they separate are tested in \S\ref{sec:multifault}--\S\ref{sec:personalwab}. On the two mid-tier models \method is top or tied-top on eight of eight cells; both losses fall on Llama 3.3 70\,B, the regime where extracted ancestor sets are larger, the single-trial counterfactual is a noisier signal, and the true fault more often lies outside the extracted DAG (a single model family, $n{=}1$; descriptive).

On synthetic \dagg tasks with known fault nodes ($n{=}60$), \method achieves 100\% top-1 attribution (MRR${=}1.0$) and 100\% repair success in \texttt{single\_fault} mode. Forward-only baselines score 0\% on this diagnostic. In \texttt{multi\_valid} mode, \method selects the minimum-cost valid repair on every trial.

The contribution of each component is summarized in two complementary ablations: Table~\ref{tab:ablations} measures the single-round attribution components on cells where the first-round repair drives the win, and Table~\ref{tab:spr-impact} (Appendix~\ref{app:spr-impl}) measures the SPR contribution on cells where the first-round MAP candidate misidentifies the fault.

\textbf{Single-round attribution components (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}).} Three components are load-bearing on the cells where the first-round repair is the deciding stage: removing the \dagg drops mean accuracy by 2.8\,pp ($-8.3$\,pp on TruthfulQA$\times$Mistral); replacing provenance-weighted priors with uniform priors drops 3.1\,pp ($-8.3$\,pp on TruthfulQA$\times$Qwen); skipping the counterfactual likelihood drops 1.5\,pp on average but 8.3\,pp on the most affected cell.

\textbf{Sequential Posterior Refinement (Appendix~\ref{app:spr-impl}, Table~\ref{tab:spr-impact}).} On the six archival-matrix cells where the verifier rejects the first-round repair, SPR contributes between $+3.3$ and $+11.7$\,pp ($+7.2$\,pp on average); pooled over that matrix it lifts \method from 84.3\% to 87.0\%, and on the primary twelve-cell rerun the same contrast appears as 85.2\% for the released no-SPR configuration versus 89.35\% SPR-enabled on identical cells. SPR derives its lift entirely from re-using the same posterior framework conditioned on the failure of the first repair (Eq.~\ref{eq:spr-update})---not from a different self-correction method---and is the methodological contrast with Reflexion (verbal critiques) vs.\ SPR (counterfactual evidence inside the same DAG-grounded posterior).

Two further findings warrant brief comment. First, increasing $M$ from 1 to 3 \emph{degrades} mean accuracy by 5.0\,pp; the full $M{\in}\{1,3,5\}$ sweep is non-monotone with a recovery at $M{=}5$ on one cell (Appendix~\ref{app:m-sweep}). The pattern is incompatible with an i.i.d.\ Bernoulli model of $r_{i,j}$ (which predicts monotone variance reduction $\propto 1/M$); we attribute it to within-candidate trial correlation (shared prompt context, shared decoding trace), so $M{=}1$ is the empirical operating point of the framework given the LLM generator's actual non-i.i.d.\ behaviour, not a statistically optimal estimator. Variance-reduced or seed-diversified counterfactual sampling are the right next steps to enable $M{>}1$ to provide net benefit. Second, the cost-aware repair objective contributes only $0.6$\,pp to raw accuracy; its operational value lies in bounded edit cost (\S\ref{sec:cost}), not in higher success rate.

\subsection{Is the Assumption-DAG Necessary? Violation-Aware Reprompt}\label{sec:vrp}

A natural alternative to the Assumption-DAG is to feed the verifier's violated profile span directly back into a re-prompt and let the agent revise its trajectory holistically, without any structured attribution. This isolates the contribution of the DAG from the contribution of \emph{having access to violation evidence at all}. We implement this as the \emph{violation-aware reprompt} (VRP) baseline: given a profile violation, format the violated profile span(s) as explicit feedback and re-prompt the agent for a single revised trajectory (one round, comparable cost to Self-Refine). \paragraph{Matched-budget control (Iter-VRP).} VRP is single-round; \method is multi-round. To isolate the DAG contribution from the retry budget, we run \emph{Iter-VRP}: DAG-free flat-text violation feedback at \method's exact retry budget, on paired examples, seeds, and initial trajectories. Iter-VRP trails or ties \method on all four ablation cells while consuming \emph{more} repair rounds: LongMemEval$\times$Llama-8B 73.3 vs.\ 88.9 ($n{=}180$, $+15.6$pp, exact McNemar $p{<}0.001$; 1.66 vs.\ 1.27 mean rounds), 2Wiki$\times$Mistral 95.0 vs.\ 99.4 ($n{=}180$, $+4.4$pp, $p{=}0.022$; 1.13 vs.\ 1.00), TruthfulQA$\times$Mistral 78.4 vs.\ 81.1 and TruthfulQA$\times$Qwen 75.0 vs.\ 75.0 (both $n{\le}37$, below the per-cell minimum detectable effect at these sizes---underpowered by construction rather than negative). Retry budget alone therefore does not account for \method's behavior: typed attribution changes \emph{where} revision is applied, not merely how often revision occurs.

\subsection{Failure-Mode Taxonomy on Ablation Cells}\label{sec:failure-taxonomy}

We use five diagnostic buckets for \method failures: (i)~verifier miss, (ii)~wrong graph edge, (iii)~wrong prior, (iv)~counterfactual repair failure, and (v)~no valid downstream trajectory after repair. The currently available repository does not contain the row-level annotations needed to certify the earlier ablation-cell failure-stage percentage, so we do not report that percentage as a result. The paired corruption diagnostic and the profile-candidacy study are reported separately because their denominators are auditable from stored rows.

\subsection{Robustness to Multiple Simultaneous Faults}\label{sec:multifault}

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Classical diagnosis and fault localization.}
\method builds directly on classical diagnosis rather than presenting fault localization as new. Model-based diagnosis~\cite{reiter1987diagnosis,dekleer1987gde} searches over candidate faulty components, with abductive and consistency-based formulations unified as points on one logical spectrum~\cite{console1991spectrum}; spectrum-based fault localization such as Tarantula and Ochiai~\cite{jones2005tarantula,abreu2006ochiai,wong2016survey} ranks components from pass/fail evidence, statistical predicate isolation scales the idea to deployed software~\cite{liblit2005scalable}, and Barinel fuses spectra with Bayesian abduction over multi-fault candidates~\cite{abreu2009barinel}. Mutation-based localization~\cite{papadakis2015metallaxis,moon2014muse} anticipates our repair-trial asymmetry: perturbing the truly faulty element flips failing checks to passing, perturbing correct elements does not. Delta debugging with its structured and state-level extensions~\cite{zeller2002delta,misherghi2006hdd,cleve2005causes}, dynamic slicing~\cite{weiser1981slicing,korel1988dynslicing,agrawal1990dynslicing}, causal reformulations of suspiciousness~\cite{halpern2005causes,baah2010causal}, and generate-and-validate repair~\cite{legoues2012genprog,mechtaev2016angelix} supply the intervention and dependency views we transfer. Our setting differs because the component set is inferred from a stochastic trajectory, only one failed execution is initially available, and repair itself supplies the counterfactual evidence. Appendix~\ref{app:classical-comparison} summarizes these correspondences.

\paragraph{Fault localization in networks and distributed systems.}
The same diagnostic discipline has a long systems lineage: risk-model and dependency-graph localization in IP and enterprise networks~\cite{kompella2005score,kandula2005shrink,bahl2007sherlock,kandula2009netmedic}, statistical problem determination over labeled request traces~\cite{chen2002pinpoint}, and the causal tracing infrastructures that make such analysis possible at scale~\cite{fonseca2007xtrace,sigelman2010dapper,kaldor2017canopy}. Provenance-based diagnosis explains observed and \emph{missing} behavior alike~\cite{wu2014negative,wu2019zeno}, differential provenance attributes faults by contrasting a good execution with a bad one~\cite{chen2016diffprov}, and counterfactual interventions on learned dependency models drive root-cause analysis in production microservices~\cite{gan2021sage}. \method imports this attribution discipline into a setting these systems never face: the dependency structure must itself be extracted from a single stochastic trajectory, and the healthy reference execution must be synthesized by repair trials rather than observed.

\paragraph{Self-correction and error localization.}
Self-Refine~\cite{madaan2023selfrefine}, Reflexion~\cite{shinn2023reflexion}, self-verification~\cite{weng2023selfverif}, LATS~\cite{zhou2024lats}, ExpeL~\cite{zhao2024expel}, AdaPlanner~\cite{sun2023adaplanner}, Tree-of-Thoughts~\cite{yao2023tot}, Graph-of-Thoughts~\cite{besta2024got}, and RAP~\cite{hao2023rap} revise or search over candidate trajectories, but do not explicitly identify which latent user assumption should be repaired. CRITIC~\cite{gou2024critic}, CoVe~\cite{dhuliawala2024cove}, SelfCheckGPT~\cite{manakul2023selfcheckgpt}, and process-reward methods~\cite{lightman2023verify,wang2023mathshepherd} provide complementary verification signals; deductive step-wise verification decomposes trajectory checking~\cite{ling2023deductive}, and semantic uncertainty offers a correction trigger~\cite{kuhn2023semantic}. Training-based routes teach correction by reinforcement or recursive introspection~\cite{kumar2024score,qu2024rise}, and search-trace training internalizes backtracking in-weights~\cite{gandhi2024sos}. Yet critical surveys find intrinsic self-correction unreliable without external feedback~\cite{kamoi2024critical}, and step-level error identification remains unsolved even in single-chain reasoning~\cite{zheng2025processbench}. Tyen et al.~\cite{tyen2024llmerrors} show that localization itself remains difficult---while correction succeeds once the error location is supplied---and He et al.~\cite{he2025selfcorrect} argue that correction should be evaluated beyond surface refinement.

\paragraph{Agent fault attribution and personalization.}
Recent systems move closer to trajectory-level diagnosis: RAFFLES~\cite{zhu2026raffles} performs iterative fault localization, ContextWeaver~\cite{wu2026contextweaver} structures interaction history as a dependency graph, Theorem-of-Thought~\cite{abdaljalil2025theoremofthought} combines abductive, deductive, and inductive reasoning, and StateAct~\cite{rozanov2025stateact} maintains forward state during agent execution. A rapidly growing attribution line complements these: taxonomies of recurring multi-agent failure modes~\cite{cemri2025mast}, annotated trace-debugging benchmarks showing LLMs localize poorly from raw traces~\cite{deshpande2025trail,chen2026traceelephant}, step-level and dependency-graph-structured attribution~\cite{zhu2026stepfinder,hou2026edge}, hypothesis- and abduction-driven attribution~\cite{qiao2026verifymas,west2025abduct}, causal blame assignment~\cite{ma2025cdcmas}, and attribution-guided reflection or counterfactual repair~\cite{wang2026doctor,bonagiri2026causalflow}. These localize or patch within multi-agent pipelines; \method instead types each assumption's provenance against a user profile, couples backward attribution to selective downstream repair, and makes the repair attempt itself the attribution evidence. This complements work on personalization-induced hallucination and constrained planning~\cite{sun2024pfqabench,xie2024travelplanner}, including LaMP~\cite{salemi2023lamp} and its long-form successor~\cite{kumar2024longlamp}, TravelAgent~\cite{chen2024travelagent}, MemoryBank~\cite{zhong2024memorybank}, and specialized planners such as ATLAS~\cite{choi2025atlas}, Behavior Forest~\cite{yuan2026behaviorforest}, and TriFlow~\cite{chen2026triflow}. Profile-conditioned generation traces to PersonaChat~\cite{zhang2018persona}; verifiable instruction constraints~\cite{zhou2023ifeval} and preference-following evaluations~\cite{zhao2025prefeval} document that stated user constraints are silently dropped within a few turns even by frontier models---the failure mode we attribute and repair; OS-style user-memory management~\cite{packer2023memgpt} and real-history web agents~\cite{cai2025personalwab} supply the deployment substrate our profile conditioning presumes.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

At $n{=}60$ per cell ($n{=}36$ for TruthfulQA's frozen held-out split), the per-cell paired-McNemar MDE at $\alpha{=}0.05$ is approximately 13\,pp; per-cell claims in this paper are therefore descriptive, with directional accuracy claims supported only at the pooled level (648 paired observations in the primary matrix). Human-annotated fault labels on natural trajectories are the highest-priority follow-up. Profile-injected variants of public benchmarks shift the construct from pure factuality to factuality-plus-profile-following, controlled within a row by paired sampling; native user-conditioned benchmarks (Appendix~\ref{app:travel}) provide a complementary evaluation track but do not reproduce the gains seen on Profile-injected QA. The relationship to Reflexion is conceptually parallel and mechanistically distinct: SPR conditions on the same DAG-grounded posterior $q$ as round one (Eq.~\ref{eq:spr-update}) and re-targets the next-best ancestor's downstream subgraph---no verbal critique, no full re-generation, no Reflexion subroutine call. The two Llama 70\,B losses (LongMemEval $-10.0$\,pp; MuSiQue $-3.4$\,pp; neither survives Holm) are consistent with an extracted-candidate limitation, but the present artifacts do not establish a unique causal explanation. Three further boundaries are reported as observed: the profile-robustness bootstrap's pre-registered bottom-decile clause was not met (pooled worst-decile mean $-0.34$\,pp; $-7.21$\,pp in the strict-fidelity confirmatory tier), single-fault baselines are archival runs subject to provider drift, and on truncated real histories \method ties rather than beats Reflexion (\S\ref{sec:personalwab}). The matched-budget Iter-VRP control is reported in §\ref{sec:vrp}.

\section*{Broader Impact}\label{sec:broader-impact}

\method makes latent agent assumptions explicit and auditable, which improves safety for user-facing deployments by exposing the inferred user attributes that drove a decision. The same representation creates a privacy surface: an \dagg materializes inferred user attributes as structured text that downstream systems may retain. Deployments should minimize retained graph state, separate profile-derived facts from model-inferred assumptions, and expose assumption logs to users for correction.

\section{Conclusion}

\method treats a failed user-conditioned trajectory as a diagnosis problem: extract the assumptions the agent relied on, organize them into a provenance-typed dependency graph, isolate the root cause by counterfactual repair trials, and regenerate only the dependent suffix, with Sequential Posterior Refinement folding each rejected repair back into the same posterior. The evaluation maps where this buys accuracy and where it does not. With a single injected fault, targeted repair holds parity-to-modest-lead over the strongest retry baseline at matched cost (89.35\% pooled over 648 observations, $+2.62$\,pp over Reflexion, descriptive). As faults compound, the mechanisms separate: over ${\sim}81{,}000$ trajectories carrying two to five simultaneous faults, \method leads all six baselines on all five datasets with margins that widen in fault count; under 100 seeded re-draws of every profile its paired advantage over Reflexion is favorable in 95 of 100 draws; and on leakage-controlled real user histories it beats direct prompting and both reprompting controls while tying Reflexion. The contribution is the closed loop---attribution evidence generated by repair execution, repair selected under edit cost, rejection re-entering the same posterior---together with an honest map of the regime in which that loop matters: multiple concurrent faults with at least one uncorrupted anchor.

\section{Classical Diagnosis Comparison}\label{app:classical-comparison}

\begin{table}[t]\centering\small
\caption{What classical diagnosis assumes, what a single LLM agent trajectory lacks, and \method's replacement.}
\label{tab:classical}
\begin{tabular}{p{3.1cm}p{3.4cm}p{2.9cm}p{3.4cm}}\toprule
Classical setting & Assumes & Our setting lacks & Replacement \\\midrule
Model-based diagnosis (Reiter; GDE) & System description given & No supplied model & LLM-extracted Assumption-DAG (\S\ref{sec:method}) \\
SBFL (Tarantula/Ochiai) & Many executions, pass/fail spectrum & One execution, one failure & Counterfactual repair trials synthesize the spectrum (Eq.~\ref{eq:repair-evidence}) \\
Delta debugging & Deterministic re-runnable oracle & Stochastic trajectory & Verifier + paired re-execution \\
Localize-then-fix pipelines & Repair follows diagnosis & --- & Repair \emph{is} the diagnostic evidence (Eqs.~\ref{eq:repair-evidence}, \ref{eq:mcgr}) \\
\bottomrule\end{tabular}\end{table}

The systems lineage makes the same correspondences concrete: Pinpoint obtains its pass/fail spectrum from live request traffic~\cite{chen2002pinpoint}, differential provenance requires a healthy reference execution that our setting must synthesize rather than observe~\cite{chen2016diffprov}, and Sage validates root causes by counterfactual intervention on a learned dependency model whose structure is given in advance---whereas the Assumption-DAG must itself be extracted from the failed trajectory~\cite{gan2021sage}.

The user profile records that the client has already renewed, prefers email-only contact, and has opted out of upsell outreach. \textbf{(1)}~The agent shortlists three follow-up actions ranked by model-inferred priors. \textbf{(2)}~Three violations are detected on the top action. \textbf{(3)}~Ancestor assumptions are traced; profile nodes ($a_1$--$a_3$) are immutable. \textbf{(4)}~Counterfactual repair trials score each candidate; the algorithm proceeds via sequential single-node repair, with re-verification detecting residual violations between passes (here resolving $a_4$ then $a_7$). \textbf{(5)}~The repaired output satisfies all profile constraints.}

\section{Algorithm and SPR Implementation Details}\label{app:spr-impl}

\paragraph{Implementation notes.} During counterfactual trials (Algorithm~\ref{alg:intro-specter}, Line~8), the repaired trajectories are cached; the final re-execution (Line~13) reuses the cached trajectory of $a^\star$ rather than regenerating it. The worked example in Figure~\ref{fig:worked-example} depicts a multi-fault scenario; in the current implementation this is handled by a sequential single-node pass: after repairing $a^\star$, re-verification may detect a residual violation triggering a second single-node repair. All hyperparameters ($M$, provenance priors, cost weights, $\delta$, $\lambda$, $T_{\text{spr}}$, $\alpha$) are fixed on Profile-X development splits before any Profile-injected-X evaluation; values are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:hparams}. The decay $\alpha$ encodes our credence that a verifier rejection is partial rather than conclusive evidence: $\alpha{\to}1$ recovers the prior (no learning from failure), $\alpha{\to}0$ recovers hard exclusion (which over-commits to ``wrong fault'' over the alternative ``right fault, bad repair''). At $T_{\text{spr}} = 2$ and our cell sizes, the second-round selector is essentially insensitive to $\alpha \in [0, 0.3]$ once the rejected candidate's mass is reduced by $10\times$ or more. The routing signal (``did $V$ accept the repaired output?'') uses no gold labels; SPR addresses what we call the \emph{single-handed} regime---failures where the first-round MAP candidate misidentifies the fault but the true fault is still inside the original ancestor set $\mathcal{C}(v;G)$.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{\method (full)}\label{alg:intro-specter}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \Require task $x$, profile $P$, trajectory $\tau$, verifier $V$, success evaluator $S$, threshold $\delta$, SPR rounds $T_{\text{spr}}$
  \Ensure  accepted, repaired, or abstained trajectory
  \State Construct \dagg $G = (A, E)$ from $\tau$.
  \State Run verifier $V(P, x, \tau, y)$.
  \If{no violation} \Return $\tau$ \EndIf
  \State $\mathcal{C}(v; G) \gets$ extracted ancestors of the violated step in $G$.
  \For{each $a_i \in \mathcal{C}(v; G)$}
    \State Sample $M$ counterfactual repairs of $a_i$.
    \State Re-execute downstream subgraph $D_i$ (cache trajectory).
    \State Compute $r_i$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:repair-evidence}), $q_i$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:attribution}).
  \EndFor
  \If{$\max_i q_i < \delta$} \Return abstain or full re-generation. \EndIf
  \State $\mathcal{T} \gets \emptyset$;\quad $q^{(0)} \gets q$.
  \For{$t = 0, 1, \ldots, T_{\text{spr}}$}
    \State Select $a^\star \gets \arg\min_{a_i \notin \mathcal{T}} \bigl[-q^{(t)}_i + \lambda\, C(a_i, G)\bigr]$;\quad $\tau^{\text{repair}} \gets$ cached trajectory for $a^\star$.
    \State Verify $\tau^{\text{repair}}$ via $V$; \textbf{if} verifier passes \textbf{then} \Return $\tau^{\text{repair}}$.
    \State $\mathcal{T} \gets \mathcal{T} \cup \{a^\star\}$;\quad update $q^{(t+1)}$ via Eq.~\ref{eq:spr-update}.
    \If{$\mathcal{T} = \mathcal{C}(v;G)$ \textbf{or} $t = T_{\text{spr}}$} \textbf{break} \EndIf
  \EndFor
  \State \Return $\tau^{\text{repair}}$ from the final round (verifier-rejected; reported as repair\_failed).
\end{algorithmic}\end{algorithm}

\section{Reproducibility Details}\label{app:repro}

\paragraph{Inference infrastructure.} All LLM calls are issued via remote provider APIs, not local GPU inference: Llama 3.3 70\,B Instruct through Together AI's hosted inference; Llama 3.1 8\,B Instruct, Mistral Nemo 12\,B Instruct, and Qwen 2.5 7\,B Instruct through OpenRouter's hosted inference. We do not maintain a local GPU cluster; the only local compute used is a single laptop (Apple Silicon) for orchestration and metric aggregation. Total API spend across all 16 cells, the synthetic diagnostics, the ablations, and the SPR overlay reruns: approximately USD 70 (cumulative across the run window of March--May 2026). The orchestration laptop ran Python 3.11 in a virtual environment; pinned package versions are in \texttt{requirements.txt} in the released code.

\paragraph{Caching and determinism.} All model calls are cached by SHA-256 hash of \texttt{(provider, model, temperature, system\_prompt, user\_prompt, max\_tokens, seed)}. Cache hits return identical bytes; the same (\texttt{task\_id}, seed) pair therefore yields identical method outputs across reruns. Cache database is shipped as a single SQLite file alongside the released code, so an external reviewer can re-execute the metric pipeline end-to-end without paying any API cost.

\paragraph{Seeds.} Example sampling uses benchmark seed 42. Each stored row is one paired task/configuration seed and McNemar tests pair on \texttt{(task\_id, seed)} without averaging stochastic outcomes into a binary observation. TruthfulQA's frozen SPR YAMLs use configuration seeds $\{42,123,456\}$; the other SPR cells use seed 42. The E2/E3 protocols introduced after review use generation seed 0 as separately frozen.

\paragraph{Inference parameters.} Temperature is 0.0 for deterministic baselines (Direct, ReAct) and for the \method extraction, verifier, counterfactual, and re-execution prompts; 0.7 for stochastic-by-design baselines (Self-Refine, Reflexion, Full-Regen, ToT). Max-tokens is 8192 for the agent prompt and trajectory regeneration calls (raised from 4096 after we observed truncation on long ALFWorld and TravelPlanner plans), 2048 for the verifier and counterfactual prompts. Default top-p is 1.0; we do not modify it.

\paragraph{Run window and orchestration.} All evaluation runs occurred between March and May 2026 against the provider models available at that time. Each cell is run by a single \texttt{run\_real\_benchmark.py} invocation that reads a YAML config (\texttt{configs/real/}), iterates over methods and seeds, and writes one JSONL row per task to \texttt{outputs/real/<dataset>\_\_<model>/}. Per-cell aggregation (\texttt{scripts/aggregate\_real\_benchmarks.py}) and p-value regeneration (\texttt{scripts/regen\_pvalue\_tables.py}) consume those JSONLs to produce the tables in this paper.

\paragraph{Repository contents.} The released code includes: full prompts (Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}), all benchmark loaders, all baseline implementations, the SPR pipeline, the verifier, the cost model, the SQLite cache file, all 16 cell YAML configs, the aggregation and regeneration scripts, the per-cell JSONL outputs that produced the headline tables, and a single notebook reproducing every figure and table in this paper.

\section{Prompts}\label{app:prompts}

Four prompts drive \method's pipeline. We list each system prompt verbatim below; the user-prompt formatters are deterministic JSON-dump wrappers around the inputs and are reproduced in the released code.

\paragraph{1. Assumption extraction.} System prompt invoked once per trajectory to construct the Assumption-DAG (\S\ref{sec:method}).

{\small\begin{verbatim}
You are extracting explicit assumptions from an agent trajectory for evaluation.

Inputs:
- User profile JSON
- Task
- Agent trajectory with steps, actions, observations, and final output

Return JSON only with:
- nodes: assumption nodes used by the agent
- edges: dependencies among assumptions
- final_decision_node: the node most directly supporting the final output

For each node, include:
- id (string, e.g. "a0", "a1", ...)
- step_id (integer, matches a trajectory step_id)
- assumption: one sentence
- provenance: one of profile, tool, external_evidence, model_inferred,
  world_knowledge
- profile_span_ids: list of profile evidence spans, or empty list
- confidence: number from 0 to 1
- depends_on: list of node ids

For each edge, include:
- source: assumption id
- target: assumption id
- type: one of supports, depends_on, contradicts, downstream_of

Do not include hidden chain-of-thought. Use concise observable reasoning
summaries only.
Output a single valid JSON object and nothing else.
\end{verbatim}}

\paragraph{2. Profile-grounded verification.} System prompt invoked to detect violations against the profile (\S\ref{sec:verifier}).

{\small\begin{verbatim}
You are a profile-grounded verifier.

Inputs:
- User profile JSON with span ids
- Task
- Agent trajectory
- Final output

Return JSON only:
{
  "pass": true or false,
  "violations": [
    {
      "violation_id": "v1",
      "step_id": integer,
      "violated_profile_span_id": "p3",
      "violated_constraint": "string",
      "trajectory_text": "string",
      "severity": "low|medium|high",
      "confidence": number from 0 to 1,
      "explanation": "one sentence"
    }
  ]
}

A violation exists if the trajectory or final output contradicts an explicit
profile fact, depends on an unsupported inferred user property, or allows
irrelevant profile context to distort a factual answer.

Output a single valid JSON object and nothing else.
\end{verbatim}}

\paragraph{3. Counterfactual repair generation.} System prompt invoked once per candidate ancestor to test whether a minimal change at that node removes the violation (\S\ref{sec:attribution}).

{\small\begin{verbatim}
You are testing whether an assumption is the root cause of a profile-grounded
error.

Inputs:
- User profile JSON
- Task
- Current Assumption-DAG
- Candidate faulty node
- Original trajectory
- Detected violation

Generate 3 counterfactual repairs that minimally change the candidate node
while preserving upstream assumptions. Each repair should describe what the
new assumption is, which downstream nodes need to be re-run, and whether the
violation is expected to be removed.

Return JSON only:
{
  "candidate_node_id": "a4",
  "repairs": [
    {
      "repair_id": "r1",
      "new_assumption": "string",
      "nodes_to_rerun": ["a4", "a5", "a6"],
      "repair_instruction": "string",
      "expected_violation_removed": true,
      "risk_notes": "string"
    }
  ]
}

Output a single valid JSON object and nothing else.
\end{verbatim}}

\paragraph{4. Downstream re-execution.} System prompt invoked once per selected repair candidate (round one and each SPR round) to regenerate only the descendants of the repaired node (\S\ref{sec:mcgr}).

{\small\begin{verbatim}
You are repairing only the downstream part of an agent trajectory.

Inputs:
- User profile JSON
- Task
- Original valid prefix steps
- Repaired assumption node
- Downstream nodes to regenerate
- Tool observations available so far

Regenerate the downstream trajectory from the repaired assumption onward.

Constraints:
- Preserve all valid prefix steps.
- Do not invent new user preferences.
- If a profile fact is missing, mark it unknown instead of assuming it.
- Use available tool observations exactly as given.
- Return structured JSON with repaired steps and final output.

Return JSON only with the schema:
{
  "repaired_steps": [{"step_id": int, "kind": "...", "text": "...",
                      "reason_summary": "..."}],
  "final_output": "string"
}

Output a single valid JSON object and nothing else.
\end{verbatim}}

User-prompt formatters for all four prompts (the deterministic JSON-dump wrappers around inputs) and the baseline-method prompts (Self-Refine critique, Reflexion reflect/retry, ReAct, Detection-Only, ToT generate/evaluate/solve, SelfCheckGPT sample/consistency/regenerate, Direct agent) are reproduced verbatim in \texttt{intro\_specter/prompts.py} in the released code.

\section{Synthetic Benchmark Details}\label{app:synthetic}
The synthetic \dagg generator constructs random DAGs of 8--15 nodes by sampling node provenances from the empirical mix observed in development trajectories, sampling edges to satisfy a target average in-degree of 2.0, and injecting one or more known faulty assumptions whose downstream effects propagate to a deterministic verifier. The verifier checks whether the trajectory output satisfies a set of compositional constraints derived from the gold graph. We generate 60 such graphs per condition. In \texttt{single\_fault}, exactly one ancestor is faulty; in \texttt{multi\_valid}, multiple ancestors can each independently resolve the violation but at different downstream edit costs.

\paragraph{Calibration to the official TravelPlanner evaluator.} We caveat the absolute numbers above. Our success criterion is a permissive subset of the official TravelPlanner evaluator: we apply only the dataset's hard-constraint predicates (budget, allergies, transportation rules, room counts, and accommodation/cuisine includes) plus our injected profile-violation predicates. We do \emph{not} apply the official evaluator's commonsense-constraint pass (e.g., realistic transit times, consistent attraction-to-attraction scheduling, no overnight transit between cities) nor the environmental-constraint pass (validity of the returned restaurants, attractions, and accommodations against the dataset's reference databases). The official evaluator's \emph{Final Pass Rate} requires all three layers (hard + commonsense + environmental) to pass simultaneously; under that stricter rubric, recent specialized planners (ATLAS~\cite{choi2025atlas}: 44.4\%, Behavior Forest~\cite{yuan2026behaviorforest}: up to 94.4\%, TriFlow~\cite{chen2026triflow}: 91.1\%) substantially outperform any general-purpose self-correction baseline including \method.

The 80.6\% Reflexion rate on Llama 8\,B in our table should not be read as a state-of-the-art TravelPlanner result---it reflects only that, on the subset of constraints we score, Reflexion's full-trajectory regeneration tends to satisfy the explicit hard constraints we retained. This appendix is included to complete the per-method comparison on user-conditioned benchmarks, not to claim a TravelPlanner accuracy result. The relevant comparison is the within-row $\Delta$ between methods evaluated on the identical predicate set; the absolute level is uncalibrated against the official benchmark.

The mean trend is decreasing in $M$ but the per-cell pattern is non-monotone, which is the diagnostic signal we discuss in §\ref{sec:ablations}. Per-cell behavior: TruthfulQA $\times$ Mistral shows a monotone decrease (86.1 $\to$ 80.6 $\to$ 72.2); TruthfulQA $\times$ Qwen drops at $M{=}3$ then plateaus; 2Wiki $\times$ Mistral is at the saturation ceiling and does not move; \textbf{LongMemEval $\times$ Llama 8B drops at $M{=}3$ then recovers to the $M{=}1$ value at $M{=}5$}. The recovery on LongMemEval is the diagnostic that rules out a simple i.i.d.\ Bernoulli interpretation: under that model, $\hat{r}_i$ would have variance $\theta_i(1-\theta_i)/M$, monotone-decreasing in $M$, and accuracy should improve (or at minimum not recover non-monotonically) as $M$ grows. The pattern is more consistent with the within-candidate trials being correlated rather than independent (shared prompt context, shared decoding trace), so larger $M$ does not realize the variance reduction the i.i.d.\ model would predict and instead introduces small misranking events on cells with closely matched priors (TruthfulQA), while having negligible net effect on cells where the prior already strongly discriminates (2Wiki and LongMemEval). Variance-reduced or seed-diversified counterfactual sampling are the natural next steps; we use $M{=}1$ as the empirical operating point of the current framework.
