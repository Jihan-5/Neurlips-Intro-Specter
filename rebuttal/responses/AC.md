# Comment to Area Chair (draft — ⟨F⟩ numbers refresh from rebuttal/tables/ at freeze)

We thank the AC and reviewers. The meta-review identifies three weaknesses as likely decision-central; this comment maps our response-period work to each. Full details and tables are in the individual replies.

**1. Realism of the evaluation (synthetic profile injection; transfer to naturally occurring failures).** We ran two new experiment families totaling ~126k evaluated trajectories across 8 dataset-conditions × 4 LLMs × 7 methods. (a) *Fault-count robustness:* extending the paper's construct to N=2–5 simultaneous faults, our method leads all six baselines on all five benchmarks (e.g., pooled 77.4 vs Reflexion's 71.2 on TwoWiki ⟨F⟩), with an explicitly-reported null on the hardest tiers where all methods tie. (b) *Real-data corruption:* three new evaluations corrupt real fields of real records (TravelPlanner native constraints, Food.com recipes, Amazon listings). These are mixed — Reflexion leads two of three (we report the losses in full, including an Amazon deficit of 11.8pp ⟨F⟩) — while our method runs at up to 1.95× lower token cost with a now-explicit, falsifiable scope: the advantage requires at least one uncorrupted anchor in the input and disappears under coordinated corruption of all mutually-supporting fields. For the profile-realism gap we additionally completed a full evaluation on PersonalWAB (WWW'25; behavior-grounded profiles from real user histories): pooled over 540 paired units (3 models × 180), our method reaches 98.0% vs Reflexion 96.3% and a budget-matched Iter-VRP 95.2%, significant against every baseline (exact McNemar p=0.035 to p<10⁻⁵; per-model breakdown and protocol notes in the Mahh reply). We explicitly concede that human-annotated fault labels on real trajectories remain camera-ready work that no existing dataset supplies.

**2. Related work and novelty relative to fault localization.** Our reply to Reviewer 4jc8 adds a dedicated subsection engaging model-based diagnosis (Reiter 1987; de Kleer & Williams 1987), spectrum-based fault localization (Jones & Harrold 2005; Abreu et al. 2006; Wong et al. 2016), delta debugging (Zeller & Hildebrandt 2002), program slicing (Weiser 1981; Korel & Laski 1988), structural causation (Halpern & Pearl 2005), and network fault localization (Kliger et al. 1995; Steinder & Sethi 2004). We identify three structural differences rather than claiming a new inference principle: the candidate set is LLM-inferred rather than given; no execution spectrum exists, so counterfactual repair trials synthesize the coverage matrix SBFL presupposes; and diagnosis and repair are a single operation selected against an edit-cost frontier. We also ran the ablation our own paper names as its highest-priority omission — Iter-VRP, a DAG-free baseline at our method's exact retry budget — on the paper's four ablation cells: our method leads significantly on the two full-n cells (+15.6pp, p<0.001; +4.4pp, p=0.022) and is tied on the two smaller pilot cells, with Iter-VRP consuming *more* observed rounds in all four (full table and protocol caveat in the 4jc8 reply) — direct evidence the retry budget alone does not explain the method's behavior.

**3. Exposition (dependency-graph figure; multiple violations).** Our reply to Reviewer LDZz clarifies that the apparent cycles in Figure 1 are backward attribution arrows, not graph edges (the DAG is acyclic by construction, with cycle-resolution logged), and commits to redrawing with distinct arrow styles; multiple-violation handling (sequential single-node repair with re-verification, already specified in Appendix A and depicted in Figure 1) will be promoted to the main method section.

**On evaluation integrity.** All response-period numbers derive from paired protocols (McNemar, paired bootstrap, Holm–Bonferroni) and are traceable to released per-example artifacts; we report nulls and losses alongside wins, including two measurement caveats we found ourselves (a scoring artifact biased against our own method, and an implementation defect in our Reflexion baseline on one cell, which we disclose rather than claim). We believe the resulting record — gains that survive harder constructions, a characterized failure boundary, engaged prior literature, and a concrete realism commitment — addresses the meta-review's concerns to the degree feasible within the response period, with the remainder specified and scheduled rather than left open.
