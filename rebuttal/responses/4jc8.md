# Response to Reviewer 4jc8 (draft — citations verified, two placeholders pending)

We thank the reviewer for this criticism, which we think is correct as stated. Abductive diagnosis, hierarchical dependency representations, and counterfactual fault isolation do have a multi-decade history in software engineering, model-based diagnosis, and network reliability, and our related work does not engage it. We are adding a dedicated subsection covering: model-based diagnosis (Reiter, 1987; de Kleer & Williams, 1987), spectrum-based fault localization (Jones & Harrold, 2005; Abreu et al., 2006; Wong et al., 2016), delta debugging (Zeller & Hildebrandt, 2002), program slicing (Weiser, 1981; Korel & Laski, 1988), structural causal accounts of actual causation (Pearl, 2009; Halpern & Pearl, 2005), and network alarm correlation and fault localization (Kliger et al., 1995; Steinder & Sethi, 2004).

On what differs, we would identify three structural properties rather than claim a new mechanism.

**The candidate set is inferred, not given.** SBFL ranks program statements, delta debugging shrinks a known input, GDE reasons over a supplied system description. In each case the component set exists prior to diagnosis. Here it does not: the assumptions an agent relied on are latent and are themselves recovered by an extractor (§2.2). Diagnosis runs over a hypothesized model, which is why extraction precision and recall (Appendix E) is a first-class metric of our pipeline and has no counterpart in classical fault localization.

**There is no spectrum, so we manufacture one.** SBFL requires many executions with pass/fail labels over shared components; delta debugging requires a deterministic re-runnable oracle over a shrinkable input. A single agent trajectory supplies neither — one execution, one failure, no repeatable decomposition. The counterfactual repair trials of Eq. 1 exist precisely to synthesize the coverage matrix that SBFL presupposes. This is the point at which the classical machinery does not transfer, and it is the honest characterization of our contribution: not a new inference principle, but a construction that makes an old one applicable to a setting that does not supply its inputs.

**Diagnosis and repair are the same operation.** SBFL emits a ranked suspiciousness list for a human developer; localization terminates before repair. In our setting the repair attempt is the likelihood proxy — a candidate scores well because repairing it removed the violation while preserving task success. Selection is then made against an edit-cost frontier (Eq. 3–4) rather than by rank alone, which is closer in spirit to fault-tolerant systems' repair-cost tradeoffs than to SBFL's diagnostic output.

We also note that one property the reviewer may read as an advantage — profile-derived nodes being immutable by construction — is the same property Reviewer Mahh identifies as a limitation under noisy or stale profiles. We agree with both readings and treat it as a modeling assumption with a stated scope, not a strength.

Finally, we ran the control our own paper names as its highest-priority omitted ablation: **Iter-VRP**, a DAG-free baseline that receives the verifier's violated-span feedback as flat text and regenerates the full trajectory, looped at exactly our method's retry budget (1 initial + 2 retries). On the paper's four ablation cells (paired examples, seeds, and initial trajectories; exact McNemar):

| Cell | n | Ours | Iter-VRP | Δ | p | mean rounds (ours / Iter-VRP) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LongMemEval × Llama-8B | 180 | 88.9 | 73.3 | +15.6 | <0.001 | 1.27 / 1.66 |
| 2Wiki × Mistral-12B | 180 | 99.4 | 95.0 | +4.4 | 0.022 | 1.00 / 1.13 |
| TruthfulQA × Mistral-12B | 37 | 81.1 | 78.4 | +2.7 | 1.0 | 1.11 / 1.46 |
| TruthfulQA × Qwen-7B | 36 | 75.0 | 75.0 | 0.0 | 1.0 | 1.42 / 1.50 |

We report observed mean rounds so the budget match is shown rather than asserted: Iter-VRP consumed *more* rounds than our method in all four cells and still trailed or tied. Two cells (run at full n=180) separate significantly; the two smaller pilot cells (n=36–37) do not. These runs use a clean-profile protocol, so absolute levels are not comparable to the submission's Table 9 — the comparison is internal and paired. We read this as direct evidence that the retry budget alone does not account for the method's behavior: typed attribution changes *where* the revision is applied, not merely *how often* revision occurs.

On the reviewer's separate point that the claims may exceed the evidence: rather than re-arguing the submitted numbers, we point to the response-period record — ~126k new evaluated trajectories across eight dataset-conditions with paired statistics, in which we report every null and loss alongside the wins (see our reply to Reviewer Mahh), and the Iter-VRP ablation above, which we ran precisely because it is the control our own paper identified as missing. We would rather the contribution be judged on that fully-traceable record than on any single headline number.
