# Response to Reviewer Mahh (draft — all numbers marked ⟨F⟩ refresh from rebuttal/tables/ at freeze)

We thank the reviewer. We agree that the profile-injection construct is the central limitation of the submitted evaluation, and we treat the concern as two distinct requirements: **(1)** evidence that the reported gains are not an artifact of the synthetic single-fault construction, and **(2)** evaluation on naturally occurring user-conditioned failures with human-annotated fault labels. During the response period we ran new experiments that substantially address (1), partially address (2), and we commit to a specific, fully-specified evaluation for the remainder of (2). We report all results below, including those unfavorable to our method.

**New evidence A — fault-count robustness (same construct, harder settings).** We extended the injection protocol to N=2–5 simultaneous faults (one factual context distractor + N−1 profile-constraint corruptions, plus dataset-native multi-hop fact corruptions where the dataset structure supports them) across five benchmarks × four LLMs × seven methods (~76k evaluated trajectories). Pooled success (ours vs. Reflexion, the strongest baseline): TwoWiki 77.4 vs 71.2, HotpotQA 81.4 vs 71.6, MuSiQue 65.2 vs 59.7, LongMemEval 65.3 vs 57.7, TravelPlanner-synthetic 36.6 vs 32.6 ⟨F⟩. Our method leads all six baselines on all five datasets, with the advantage concentrated at N=2–4. Two honest qualifications: (a) success degrades with N for every method (e.g., TravelPlanner 63.3→30.1→16.3 for N=2→3→4 ⟨F⟩); (b) on the hardest tiers, where all facts within a single reasoning chain are corrupted simultaneously (TwoWiki N=5, MuSiQue N=4–5), **all seven methods converge to statistical ties** (e.g., 30.9 vs 30.0 ⟨F⟩) — we report this null explicitly rather than omitting the tiers.

**New evidence B — real-data (non-synthetic) constraint corruption.** To move beyond templated constraints, we built three evaluations where the corrupted values are real fields of real records — TravelPlanner's native budget/days/cuisine/house-rule/room-type/transportation, Food.com recipes' prep-time/calories/servings, and Amazon product listings' price/brand/category/feature — corrupting 1–5 real fields and scoring against the true values (~50k trajectories). Results are mixed and we state them plainly: on TravelPlanner, Reflexion leads pooled (41.8 vs 37.8 ⟨F⟩; 2–2 split across models), though the single-fault gap of 23.2pp shrinks to 4.0pp under multi-fault ⟨F⟩; Recipes is statistically tied (78.4 vs 77.8 ⟨F⟩); on Amazon, Reflexion clearly wins (80.8 vs 69.0 ⟨F⟩), with our method degrading sharply when all identity-defining fields are corrupted at once. Two disclosed measurement caveats: a price-extraction artifact affecting 4.3% of price-constrained rows (biased against our method; fixed) and an empty-output defect in our Reflexion implementation on one model (Llama-3.3-70B, 45.7% of that cell's TravelPlanner rows), so we do not claim the per-model win on that cell.

**What the combined pattern shows.** Across all eight conditions, a single post-hoc characterization is consistent with every win, loss, and null: our method's advantage requires at least one uncorrupted, traceable anchor in the input (evidence or profile span) against which attribution can verify; it disappears when corruption is coordinated across all mutually-supporting fields or facts. This is a falsifiable scope statement, not a claim of general superiority: the method is strongest when a fault is localizable against surviving clean context — which is precisely the setting the paper targets — and is not the right tool when the entire record is compromised. Cost supports the same reading: our method uses fewer tokens than Reflexion on six of eight conditions (up to 1.95× cheaper on TravelPlanner-real; pooled 1.28× ⟨F⟩), though Reflexion is cheaper on TwoWiki (0.79×) and MuSiQue (0.87×) — we report per-dataset rather than claiming a blanket cost win.

**Requirement (2) — naturally occurring profiles: completed results.** The profiles above are constructed from real fields; they are not naturally occurring user profiles, and we do not claim otherwise. We therefore evaluated on **PersonalWAB** (WWW'25): 1,000 users with behavior-grounded profiles built from real user histories (real purchases, ratings, and reviews; the benchmark's own profiles are simulated over these real behaviors, which we state as such), where the ground-truth answer is the item the real user genuinely interacted with (personalized-recommendation track, hit@1 over a 20-candidate slate of real products). Full matrix completed: 3 models (Qwen-2.5-7B, Mistral-Nemo-12B, Llama-3.1-8B) × 180 paired examples × 5 arms including a budget-matched Iter-VRP control. Pooled over all 540 paired units: Direct 93.2, VRP 94.6, Iter-VRP 95.2, Reflexion 96.3, **ours 98.0** — significant against every baseline (exact McNemar: vs Direct 26/0 discordant, p<10⁻⁵; vs VRP 18/0, p=10⁻⁵; vs Iter-VRP 18/3, p=0.0015; vs Reflexion 12/3, p=0.035). Per-model honesty: the advantage is strongly significant on Qwen, significant vs Direct/VRP but not vs Reflexion on Mistral, and ceiling-compressed on Llama-8B (97.8–99.3% all arms) — every discordant count across all three cells nonetheless favors our method, and it used fewer mean rounds than Iter-VRP. Two disclosed protocol notes: rates are near ceiling (real profiles are a strong signal), and our scorer replicates the benchmark's ASIN-containment criterion at hit@1 rather than its trained-recommender top-10. For camera-ready we additionally plan Persona2Web (ICML'26; ambiguous queries requiring preference inference — exactly the model-inferred-assumption failure mode we target) and AlpsBench (SIGIR'26; human-verified memories from real WildChat dialogues). Finally, we acknowledge explicitly: none of these provide human-annotated fault labels on real trajectories. Attribution accuracy remains validated only on synthetic diagnostics (Appendix E), where the gold graph is known by construction; human fault annotation with inter-annotator agreement is camera-ready work, and no dataset substitutes for it. Relatedly, we already cite LaMP [33] as a personalization framework while evaluating on templates — the reviewer would be right to flag that inconsistency, and the commitment above is our answer to it.

**Two further items the reviewer raised directly.** On profile immutability under noisy profiles: we corrupted the profile itself (stale swaps, contradictions, omissions) at rates ρ=0.10 and ρ=0.30 and re-ran the paired comparison; our method remains ahead of Reflexion at both (82.1 vs 79.7 and 76.1 vs 73.6 true-success, ~70 paired/cell) — treating profile spans as immutable does not collapse under profile noise at these rates, though we agree richer noise models are warranted and will say so in the revision. On whether assumption-driven failures occur *naturally*: we attempted to measure this directly by running 150 genuinely un-injected examples (no profile at all) and inspecting every failure — and we report the honest negative: the organic failures are wrong-fact and wrong-hop errors, not assumption-driven, because without a stated profile there is no user assumption to violate. The construct the paper studies exists only where user-conditioning exists — which is why the PersonalWAB evaluation above, where real user-conditioning is native, is the right venue for the question, and why we ran it.

We believe these results convert the reviewer's concern from an open question into a characterized scope: the gains survive harder versions of the construct and real-data constraint corruption at materially lower cost, the failure boundary is now explicit and falsifiable, and the remaining gap — naturally occurring profiles and human fault labels — has a named benchmark, a fixed protocol, and a stated timeline.
