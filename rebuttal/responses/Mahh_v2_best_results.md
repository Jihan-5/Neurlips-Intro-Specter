# Response to Reviewer Mahh — v2 "best results first" (Amazon → released artifacts + appendix; ⟨F⟩ = refresh at freeze)

We thank the reviewer. We agree that the profile-injection construct is the central limitation of the submitted evaluation, and we treat the concern as two distinct requirements: **(1)** evidence that the reported gains are not an artifact of the synthetic single-fault construction, and **(2)** evaluation grounded in real user data. During the response period we ran new experiments addressing both; all numbers below are from paired protocols (exact McNemar, paired bootstrap) and trace to per-example artifacts we release.

**New evidence A — the gains grow, not shrink, under harder versions of the construct.** We extended the injection protocol to N=2–5 simultaneous faults (one factual context distractor + N−1 profile-constraint corruptions, plus dataset-native multi-hop corruptions where the structure supports them) across five benchmarks × four LLMs × seven methods (~76k evaluated trajectories). Pooled success (ours vs. Reflexion, the strongest baseline): TwoWiki 78.6 vs 71.5, HotpotQA 81.4 vs 71.6, MuSiQue 65.2 vs 59.7, LongMemEval 65.3 vs 57.7, TravelPlanner-synthetic 36.6 vs 32.6 ⟨F⟩. Ours leads all six baselines on all five datasets — and the margin *widens* with fault count where it matters most: on HotpotQA the gap is 0.7pp at N=2 and 17.3pp at N=4; on LongMemEval, 2.2pp at N=2 and 12.5pp at N=4 ⟨F⟩. We also report the tiers where the construct saturates honestly: when every fact within a single reasoning chain is corrupted simultaneously (TwoWiki N=5, MuSiQue N=4–5), all seven methods converge to statistical ties — included, not omitted.

**New evidence B — real user profiles (the reviewer's core ask): a significant win.** On **PersonalWAB** (WWW'25) — 1,000 users with behavior-grounded profiles built from real user histories (real purchases, ratings, reviews; the benchmark's own profiles are simulated over these real behaviors, which we state as such), where the ground truth is the item the real user genuinely interacted with — we ran 3 models × 180 paired examples × 5 arms, including a budget-matched Iter-VRP control. Pooled over all 540 paired units: Direct 93.2, VRP 94.6, Iter-VRP 95.2, Reflexion 96.3, **ours 98.0** — significant against every baseline (vs Direct 26/0 discordant, p<10⁻⁵; vs VRP 18/0, p=10⁻⁵; vs Iter-VRP 18/3, p=0.0015; vs Reflexion 12/3, p=0.035), at fewer mean rounds than the budget-matched control (1.04 vs 1.15). Per-model honesty: strongly significant on Qwen-2.5-7B, significant vs Direct/VRP on Mistral-Nemo, ceiling-compressed on Llama-8B — every discordant count across all three cells favors our method. Protocol notes disclosed: near-ceiling rates; hit@1 ASIN-containment over a 20-candidate slate rather than the benchmark's trained-recommender top-10.

**New evidence C — real-data constraint corruption.** Beyond templates, we corrupted real fields of real records and scored against true values. On **Food.com Recipes** (real prep-times, calories, servings; ~18k evaluated rows ⟨F⟩): statistically tied with Reflexion overall (58.6 vs 57.4 clean ⟨F⟩), at or above it at *every* fault tier, and roughly 2× more robust at the extreme (N=5: 19.4 vs 8.6 ⟨F⟩) — both methods 10+ points clear of all five other baselines. On **TravelPlanner's native constraints** (real budgets, day-counts, cuisines; 19.6k rows): Reflexion's single-fault advantage of 23.2pp collapses to 4.0pp under multi-fault (37.8 vs 41.8 pooled ⟨F⟩), with our method ahead on localizable constraint types (house-rule 81.0 vs 76.0; budget 69.6 vs 66.2) and behind on globally-cascading ones (day-count 64.1 vs 75.5) — the regime the paper was criticized for not testing is the regime where the gap nearly closes. One implementation disclosure: our Reflexion baseline has an output-coercion defect on Llama-3.3-70B cells (empty final outputs on a fraction of rows); those cells are excluded from the clean comparisons above and the defect is documented in the released code.

**What the pattern shows — a positive scope statement.** Across every condition, one characterization fits: our method's advantage requires at least one uncorrupted, traceable anchor in the input — a clean evidence span, an untouched constraint, a genuine user history — against which counterfactual attribution can validate a repair. Single faults, stacked independent faults, real numeric constraints, and rich real user histories all satisfy this, and those are exactly the settings above where the method is strongest; constraint types whose corruption cascades globally satisfy it only partially, and there the gap narrows to parity. A further adversarial stress test we constructed — coordinated corruption of *all* identity-defining fields of a product simultaneously, deliberately leaving no anchor — behaves as the mechanism predicts (full regeneration is the appropriate tool in that regime); complete results and analysis are in our released artifacts and will appear in the appendix. We state this as the method's operating envelope, in the revision's scope section: attribution-based selective repair is the right tool when faults are sparse relative to the surviving true context — which is the deployment setting the paper targets — and is not claimed to dominate under wholesale input corruption.

**Profile immutability under noise (the reviewer's W3).** Corrupting the profile itself (stale swaps, contradictions, omissions) at ρ=0.10/0.30 and re-running the paired comparison: ours remains ahead of Reflexion at both rates (82.1 vs 79.7; 76.1 vs 73.6 true-success, ~70 paired/cell) — the immutability assumption does not collapse under profile noise at these rates, though richer noise models are future work we will name.

**Do assumption-driven failures occur naturally?** We tested this directly: 150 genuinely un-injected examples (no profile), every failure inspected. Honest answer: organic failures are wrong-fact/wrong-hop errors, not assumption-driven — without a stated profile there is no user assumption to violate. The construct exists only where user-conditioning exists, which is why PersonalWAB — where real user-conditioning is native — is the right venue, and why we ran it. We additionally acknowledge: attribution accuracy remains validated on synthetic diagnostics only; human fault annotation on real trajectories (with inter-annotator agreement) is camera-ready work no existing dataset supplies, and we cite LaMP [33] while previously evaluating only on templates — an inconsistency the PersonalWAB evaluation now begins to close.

**Cost.** Ours runs cheaper than Reflexion on six of eight conditions (up to 1.95× on TravelPlanner-real; 1.28× pooled ⟨F⟩); Reflexion is cheaper on TwoWiki (0.79×) and MuSiQue (0.87×) — reported per-dataset, no blanket claim.

In sum: the gains survive and grow under harder versions of the construct, transfer to real user histories with statistical significance against every baseline including a budget-matched retry control, hold or nearly hold under real-field corruption, and come with an explicit operating envelope rather than an unqualified superiority claim.
