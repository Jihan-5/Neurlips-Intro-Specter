We thank the reviewer for this criticism, which we believe is correct as stated: abductive diagnosis, hierarchical dependency representations, and counterfactual fault isolation have a multi-decade history in software engineering, model-based diagnosis, and network reliability, and our related work does not engage it. We respond in three parts: the literature we will add, what is structurally different in our setting, and a new control experiment run for this response.

**1. The literature we are adding.** A dedicated related-work subsection covering: **model-based diagnosis** — Reiter's theory of diagnosis from first principles (AIJ 32:57–95, 1987) and de Kleer & Williams' GDE for multiple faults (AIJ 32:97–130, 1987 — the two papers are adjacent in the same volume), whose minimal-conflict-set reasoning is the closest ancestor of our candidate-ancestor formulation; **spectrum-based fault localization** — Tarantula (Jones & Harrold, ASE 2005), the Ochiai coefficient (Abreu et al., PRDC 2006), and the Wong et al. survey (TSE 2016), whose suspiciousness-ranking-over-components view maps onto our attribution score $q$; **delta debugging** (Zeller & Hildebrandt, TSE 2002) as input-space fault isolation; **program slicing** (Weiser, ICSE 1981; Korel & Laski, IPL 1988), whose dependency-closure semantics parallel our downstream-subgraph re-execution $D_i$; **structural accounts of actual causation** (Halpern & Pearl, BJPS 2005; Pearl 2009), which formalize the but-for reasoning our counterfactual trials operationalize; and **network fault localization** (Kliger et al., IM 1995; Steinder & Sethi, SCP 2004), where symptom-to-cause decoding under noise anticipates our violation-to-ancestor inference.

**2. What differs — three structural properties, not a new inference principle.**

| Classical setting | What it assumes | What our setting lacks | Our replacement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Model-based diagnosis (GDE) | System description given | No supplied model | LLM-extracted Assumption-DAG (§2.2) |
| SBFL (Tarantula/Ochiai) | Many executions, pass/fail spectrum | One execution, one failure | Counterfactual repair trials synthesize the spectrum (Eq. 1) |
| Delta debugging | Deterministic re-runnable oracle | Stochastic trajectory | Verifier + paired re-execution |
| Localize-then-fix pipelines | Repair follows diagnosis | — | Repair *is* the diagnostic evidence (Eqs. 1, 3–4) |

*(a) The candidate set is inferred, not given.* SBFL ranks statements of a known program; delta debugging shrinks a known input; GDE reasons over a supplied system description. In every classical setting the component set exists prior to diagnosis. Here, the assumptions an agent relied on are latent — they are recovered by an LLM extractor (§2.2), and diagnosis runs over a *hypothesized* model. This is why extraction quality is a first-class metric in our pipeline (Appendix E: node precision/recall .94/.91, edge .88/.82, cycle rate 5%) with no counterpart in classical fault localization: if extraction is wrong, diagnosis is wrong about the wrong model, a failure mode Reiter's framework never faces.

*(b) There is no execution spectrum, so we synthesize one.* SBFL's suspiciousness scores require many executions with pass/fail labels over shared components; delta debugging requires a deterministic, re-runnable oracle over a shrinkable input. A single agent trajectory supplies neither: one execution, one failure, no repeatable decomposition. The counterfactual repair trials of Eq. 1 exist precisely to synthesize the coverage matrix that SBFL presupposes — each trial is a constructed (execution, outcome) pair for one candidate. We believe this is the honest characterization of our contribution: not a new inference principle, but a construction that makes a classical principle applicable to a setting that does not supply its inputs.

*(c) Diagnosis and repair are one operation.* SBFL emits a ranked list for a human developer; localization terminates before repair. In our setting the repair attempt *is* the likelihood evidence — a candidate scores well because repairing it removed the violation while preserving task success — and selection trades fault probability against an edit-cost frontier over the downstream subgraph (Eq. 3–4), closer in spirit to fault-tolerant systems' repair-cost tradeoffs than to SBFL's diagnostic output. Consistent with this coupling, our failure taxonomy (§4.5) shows that on the ablation cells 100% of our failures are repair-stage rather than localization-stage — the classical decomposition of "localize, then fix" does not describe where this system succeeds or fails.

**3. New control: the ablation our own paper called missing.** §4.4 names a DAG-free iterative baseline at matched retry budget ("Iter-VRP") as the highest-priority ablation we did not run — precisely the control that separates "typed attribution does work" from "more retries do work." We ran it during the response period on the paper's four ablation cells (paired examples, seeds, and initial trajectories; exact McNemar; observed mean rounds reported so the budget match is shown, not asserted):

| Cell | n | Ours | Iter-VRP | Δ (pp) | p | Mean rounds (ours / Iter-VRP) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LongMemEval × Llama-8B | 180 | 88.9 | 73.3 | +15.6 | <0.001 | 1.27 / 1.66 |
| 2Wiki × Mistral-12B | 180 | 99.4 | 95.0 | +4.4 | 0.022 | 1.00 / 1.13 |
| TruthfulQA × Mistral-12B | 37 | 81.1 | 78.4 | +2.7 | 1.0 | 1.11 / 1.46 |
| TruthfulQA × Qwen-7B | 36 | 75.0 | 75.0 | 0.0 | 1.0 | 1.42 / 1.50 |

Iter-VRP consumed *more* rounds in all four cells and trailed or tied; the two full-n cells separate significantly, while the two TruthfulQA cells (n=36–37) sit well below the ~13pp per-cell minimum detectable effect our own §6 reports at these sizes — underpowered by construction rather than negative. (These runs use a clean-profile protocol, so absolute levels are not comparable to Table 9; the comparison is internal and paired.) The same control also appears as an arm in our new real-user-profile evaluation — PersonalWAB (WWW'25): 1,000 users with behavior-grounded profiles from real purchase/rating/review histories, ground truth being the item the real user genuinely chose; three LLMs × 180 paired examples × 5 arms:

| Arm (540 paired units) | Success (%) | Discordant (ours-win/loss) | exact McNemar p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct | 93.2 | 26 / 0 | $3.0{\times}10^{-8}$ |
| VRP (single-round) | 94.6 | 18 / 0 | $7.6{\times}10^{-6}$ |
| Iter-VRP (matched budget) | 95.2 | 18 / 3 | 0.0015 |
| Reflexion | 96.3 | 12 / 3 | 0.035 |
| **Ours** | **98.0** | — | — |

Ours again leads the budget-matched control at fewer mean rounds (1.04 vs 1.15). We read the two results together as direct evidence that retry budget alone does not account for the method's behavior: typed attribution changes *where* revision is applied, not merely how often revision occurs. A per-example view sharpens this: 18 of the 540 PersonalWAB units are failed by all three revision baselines — VRP, Iter-VRP, and Reflexion — together (Direct solves none of them either). Our method solves 10 of those 18, while the reverse (some revision baseline succeeds where ours fails) occurs on only 3 units in the entire benchmark. At these counts this is consistent with differential targeting rather than decisive for it — but the asymmetry is the pattern targeting predicts.

**4. One property worth flagging as double-edged.** Profile-derived nodes are immutable by construction — arguably a strength (the user's stated constraints are never rewritten to excuse a failure), and identified by Reviewer Mahh as a limitation under noisy or stale profiles. We think both readings are correct and treat it as a modeling assumption with a stated, tested scope: under direct profile corruption at $\rho=0.10/0.30$ the paired advantage over Reflexion persists (82.1 vs 79.7; 76.1 vs 73.6), and richer noise models are named future work.

**5. On claims versus evidence.** Rather than re-argue the submitted numbers, we point to the response-period record: ~140k newly evaluated paired trajectories across eight dataset-conditions, with nulls and losses reported alongside wins (including a real-field corruption domain where Reflexion leads us 40.5 vs 32.8), and the control above, run because our own paper identified it as missing. The revision tempers the contribution statement concretely, not as a category: contribution (i) in §1 — "the Assumption-DAG + Intro-Specter framework that performs typed fault attribution and selective subgraph repair" — becomes "a construction that transfers classical abductive diagnosis to LLM agent trajectories by synthesizing the execution spectrum classical methods presuppose," and the abstract's framing sentence is revised to match. The claim is integration made non-trivial by the setting, not a new diagnostic principle.
